Top-down pixel grass: tiny algorithms for trampled grass, swaying fields, butterflies, fireflies and small life

A field looks alive when it moves a little, reacts when you pass and fills with small creatures at the right hour. The classic answers are tiny. Pokémon hides your legs with one priority bit. Tibia regrows cut grass with a decay timer. Stardew keeps its tufts still until something touches them, and Terraria adds one cosine wave per column. This study collects those rules, adds trail maps, regrowth, server-side hiding, butterflies and fireflies that fall into step, and measures what each one costs. Draw calls decide the price of grass, not pixels. Aimed at Godot 4 on the web and a server-authoritative MMO.
