Simulation

1 · Server spell events

The server decides hits and conditions. It sends five kinds of small events: cast start (caster, spell, element, cast time), missile (from tile, to tile, missile id), effect (tile, effect id), area (origin, mask id, effect id, delay per ring) and condition on or off. Effect is 9 bytes and missile 11 bytes, the Tibia 12+ sizes read from TFS. The cast, area mask and condition sizes are illustrative. Tibia sends an area as one effect per tile, so a 37-tile meteor storm costs 333 bytes per spectator. Here it is one mask event of about 11 bytes, and the HUD shows both totals. The delay field follows Old School RuneScape: one event arrives early and the client plays the rings later. A cap or a tier rule can never change who got hit. Server events view draws each event where it happened and lists the log.

2 · Spells as data

A spell is a table of phases, not code: {id:'FIREBOLT', el:fire, cast:0.30, travel:{kind:'homing', spr:0}, impact:{dmg:18, decal:'scorch'}, cond:{k:'burn', dur:3}}. Each element owns a 5-step ramp from a dark hue-shifted edge to a light core, and a shape. Fire rises in tongues. Ice is shards and straight spikes. Energy is jagged. Poison bubbles slowly. Holy uses rings and rays. Arcane uses orbits and sigils. Pure white is a sixth entry used only on the peak frame, and nothing is pure black (Riot's guide: avoid 100% and 0% values). Sprites are stored as ramp indices, so the colour-blind palette is one table swap. In that mode every hostile spell and telegraph is orange, your shield and heal stay blue and grey, and status icons add a shape per condition. Orange against blue is the safe pair in the Game Accessibility Guidelines. Loudness follows Riot's importance scale: the shield is defensive, the fire bolt is damage, the meteor storm is the ultimate.

3 · Cast and charge

The cast is the anticipation. A rune ring draws in under the caster over the cast time: outer ring, inner ring, rune marks, then the element's sigil polygon. It turns at 40 degrees per second, takes the element's colour and is sized to the spell's area, as in Ragnarok Online, so other players can read what is coming. The meteor circle spans its 7-tile mask. Particles gather toward the staff hand and a charge orb grows there. On release the circle shows pure white for one beat and dims through the ramp. A monster's cast puts a red diamond under you. That telegraph is drawn last, at full opacity, and is never evicted. Guild Wars 2 had to patch hidden telegraphs three weeks after it let players hide ally effects.

4 · Projectiles and trails

Missiles are pre-drawn per direction and move on whole pixels. 8 directions is the OTClient way, a 3 by 3 grid of frames that is never rotated. Run-time rotation spins one sprite by the exact angle. Watch the outline break and crawl on the curving fire bolt. Flight time is 150 ms times the square root of the distance in tiles (OTClient), so 9 tiles take 450 ms. The fire bolt launches off-axis, turns from 3.5 degrees per frame upward and is pulled onto the target over the last 40% of its flight, so it lands on the server's clock. The ice shard flies straight and can miss. Poison is lobbed, with a ground shadow that marks the landing tile. The beam is a channel with nested layers around a light core. Chain lightning is midpoint displacement over 5 generations, 32 segments, with two branches, re-rolled every 3 frames for 300 ms. It peaks once and does not strobe. Trails are a ring buffer: an 8-point ribbon (Mindustry keeps 7 to 9), 5 dimmed afterimages (Terraria), or a three-layer particle wake with lifetimes of 0.1, 0.6 and 1.4 s (Noita).

5 · Impacts and areas

A hit is a 5-frame flipbook at 75 ms per frame, OTClient's effect clock: white flash, hot core, broken ring, sparks, dark fragments. The climax shape lasts 2 frames, so the target is hidden for 150 ms at most. Front-loaded sparks plays the same frames at 33, 33, 50, 83 and 134 ms, the fast-then-slow timing of fighting-game sparks. Area spells draw one effect per tile over a tile mask. The meteor storm uses Tibia's 37-tile circle, ring by ring at 55 ms per ring, or all tiles in phase as Tibia fires them. A 200 ms shockwave ring thins out as it grows. Residue stays behind: scorch for 12 s and fields in 3 shrinking stages of 4 s, animated at 500 ms per frame like Tibia field items. Both are compressed. Mindustry keeps scorch for 60 s and a Tibia fire field lasts 3 times 120 s. A tile holds one field and a new one refreshes it.

6 · Auras and status

Each state has four slots, as in Diablo II: a back overlay drawn before the character, a front overlay drawn after it, a one-shot burst when it starts and a puff when it ends. The shield is a rim-only bubble, so the mage stays readable. A hit lights the rim on the side it came from, and the shield ends with a break ring. Status is one multiply colour per sprite, baked once and cached (the Ragnarok Online mechanism), plus a small overlay: flames, bubbles, ice shards at the feet, arcs. One tint shows at a time, by priority. The shocked tint blinks at 3 Hz and the target marker pulses at 2.5 Hz after a 250 ms lock-on spin. Orbit radius and period, the afterimage count and the bubble design are illustrative.

7 · Glow and blending

Palette ramp uses no alpha. Effects are opaque ramp colours, and a 80 by 45 light map raises the floor in dithered steps. Additive adds every effect pixel to what is below and draws a smooth disc per emitter. It looks soft, washes out in a crowd and costs the most. Low-res buffer sums emissive pixels into 4 px blocks, blurs them twice and adds them back at a fixed cost. Missiles, sparks and fields all feed the light map, so they light the courtyard at dusk and night. Diablo II gave 318 of its 742 missile types a light. Dim scene for ultimate is the contrast trick from Pokemon Emerald: the floor blends 7/16 of the way to black, which leaves 9/16 of its brightness, while your own meteor storm plays.

8 · Screen feedback

A big hit on you tints the screen edge in the element's colour for 0.3 s at 50% strength, with a dithered border. Low health pulses the edge at 0.5 Hz. Your own meteor storm flashes the whole screen once, in a light cream, never saturated red. The limiter follows WCAG 2.3.1: at most 3 flashes per second. A flash that is not your own ultimate is held to 25% of the screen. Flashes under about 1,600 px, 6 tiles here, are exempt. Extra flashes turn into a border. Zelda 3's Ether medallion flashed the background at 7.5 Hz, which this limiter would stop. Reduce flashing turns every flash into a border and removes the white lightning frame.

9 · Clarity manager

Every effect has an owner tier: own, party, others, hostile to me. Own and hostile effects draw in full. Party effects lose 30% of their particles and all screen feedback. Others are set by you. Reduced draws them at 40% opacity by ordered dither with 50% of the particles, small sparks without the white first frame, every second area tile, no glow, no decals. Minimal keeps a dot for the missile and a 3-frame spark. The three opacity sliders mirror the ones Tibia announced in October 2025. A cap of 96 live effects evicts by priority: own, then hostile, then party, then others, residue before missiles, oldest first. A second spark of the same kind on the same tile within 150 ms is merged. Particles over the cap are refused, as Terraria refuses dust. Set the crowd to 30, raise stress, and switch the manager off to see what it saves.

What it costs

Every layer is timed with performance.now() each frame and smoothed. All effects write into one 320 by 180 pixel buffer. Particles live in four pooled struct-of-arrays ranges, one per layer, and are never allocated during play. One-shot effects are functions of a seed and a time, with no per-pixel state. Cost view counts effect pixel writes per screen pixel. A pixel written 8 times was paid for 8 times and shown once. The particle cap, damage values, cast times and cooldowns are illustrative.
