NPC Dialog Sandbox

Kingsfoil Studios · Playable companion to the top-down pixel NPCs and dialog study · Game feel series #1 · November 2026
loading

Walk with the arrows or WASD, or tap a tile. Tap or face an NPC and press E, Space or Enter to talk and to advance. Up/Down pick a choice. L dialog log, 1-3 dialog style (0 per NPC), V next view, P pause, Esc close. The elder wants herbs, Mara runs the shop, the sailor answers keywords. Tiles are 16 px here, half of Asterion's 32 px. Ranges, rates and byte sizes are illustrative.

1 · Server conversation truth

The server owns every dialog graph and the quest state. Each player has a session (npc, node, variables). The client sends intents like talk, opt 1 (4 bytes) or kw "travel". The server answers with line ids and arguments. A Tibia-style text line costs 18 bytes plus name and text, a line id costs 8 bytes plus 2 and the name per name argument. Open the Server sessions view to watch sessions, the sailor's focus queue and packets in flight.

2 · Channels and focus

Say reaches a box of 8 by 6 tiles around the speaker and yell 18 by 14, as in TFS. Whisper reaches 1 tile. You only receive lines from inside those boxes. Public chat has a 4 message bucket with one credit back every 1.5 s, and each overflow mutes the talker for 5·n² s. NPC input has its own bucket of 3 per second with a burst of 5 (estimate). The conversation range is 3 tiles. The sailor uses old TFS 0.3 shared focus: he talks to whoever said hi first and tells the next talker to wait. Private sessions is TFS 1.x multi-focus: everyone gets private replies, he faces the first talker still in focus, and other players' conversations are hidden from you.

3 · Authoring

All lines come from a Yarn-like script in this file: title: nodes, * option -> node, <<if $herbs >= 3>>, <<set>>, keyword blocks and bark tables. Every line carries a #l:id tag that keys a string table with an English and a Spanish column. Inline tags drive colour, wave, shake, pauses and speed.

4 · Acting in the world

NPCs turn to the talker, bob while idle and pop emote icons with a short squash. Quest markers bob above heads: gold ! for an offer, a hollow grey ? while in progress, solid gold ? when ready to turn in, so the state never depends on colour alone. A prompt appears when you face an NPC within one tile, or across the shop counter.

5 · Overhead text

Bubbles are a 9-slice frame with a tail and a 3x5 pixel font. They live max(3 s, 60 ms per character), twice as long for yells, as in OTClient. Each speaker keeps at most 3. NPC replies are cyan outlined text and your own lines are yellow in the log. With 30 players the clutter rule decides what you can read: overlap, stack upward, a priority order with a cap of 12, or collapse distant chat into the log. Bubbles of speakers off screen stick to the edge with an arrow.

6 · Dialog UI

The box has a 32 px portrait (64 px at Asterion scale) with blinks, mouth flaps and expressions. The typewriter runs at 20 characters per second by default. It waits 5 glyph-times after , ; : and 10 after . ! ?. First press completes the page. After that a 200 ms guard eats early presses (each one shaves 50 ms), then the next press advances. The arrow bobs 0, 1, 2, 1 px, 9 frames per step. Boxes open and close in 8 frames. Bubble mode keeps the talk over the NPC's head. Keyword chat is Tibia's console plus an overhead line, and keywords are clickable.

7 · Panels and handoff

A quest offer becomes a card with reward icons. Mara hands off to a trade panel and takes the talk back when you close it. Turn-ins show a reward popup and update the tracker. Walk more than 3 tiles away and the NPC says "Goodbye." and closes the conversation.

8 · Ambient life

Three villagers follow schedules on a 2 minute day. Within 3 tiles they turn their heads and bark, with a 12 s cooldown each and 4 s across the town. A bark never starts during your conversation. Their lines change after you help the elder.

9 · Text cost and access

Glyphs are blitted from a pre-rendered atlas, one drawImage per glyph, with shadow and outline baked in. Canvas fillText draws the same layout with vector text so you can compare cost and crispness. Blips are a WebAudio square wave every second letter, pitched per NPC. Speed presets, auto-advance, 2x text and reduce motion cover access.
