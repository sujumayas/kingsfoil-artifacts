Top-down pixel combat juice: hit feedback, targeting and aimed skills for a 2D MMORPG

A Tibia hit is a coloured number that rises 48 px in one second, a one-tile effect at 75 ms per frame and a server that decides everything. Action games add freezes, flashes and shake, and much of that breaks when 30 players share a screen. A global freeze stops everyone's world, and a stranger's spell shakes your camera. This study collects what still works in an MMO. That means a swing with a held end pose, hitstop only on fights you are in, a 100 ms flash, shake for your own camera, numbers that merge and cap, targeted skills that cannot miss and aimed ones that can. Each comes with its server and latency rules. We measured what juice costs, and it is cheap if it batches. Aimed at Godot 4 on the web and a server-authoritative MMO.
