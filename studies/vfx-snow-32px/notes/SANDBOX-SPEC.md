# Snow 32 Sandbox spec (shared by the report writer and the sandbox builder)

The sandbox plays the recommended architecture of the report's §7. The report's §8 describes it. Both follow this file.
If research shows a number below is wrong, the lead or the report writer corrects it here under "Corrections" and messages the sandbox builder. Numbers marked (illustrative) are placeholders until research replaces them.

The study has two equal goals: ARTISTIC QUALITY and RUNTIME EFFICIENCY. The scene must look like a game an artist drew at 32 px, and the sandbox must show what every layer costs at 4 times the pixels, live.

The point of the sandbox: the same snow logic as `studies/vfx-snow/sandbox.html` (read it first, it is the engine to port), at the scale of the real game. 32x32 px tiles, characters about 24x32 px.

## Page
- `studies/vfx-snow-32px/sandbox.html`, one hand-written file, no external assets, no external fonts or images.
- Native canvas `<canvas id="c" width="640" height="360">`, scaled with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Whole-number scale where the stage is at least 640 px wide; on narrower screens the canvas shrinks to fit (a 390 px phone shows it below 1x, which is accepted) with no horizontal overflow and no console errors.
- Page chrome, HUD style, control layout, explainer cards, the `fit()` whole-number scaling and keyboard handling copied from `studies/vfx-magic/sandbox.html` (the newest sandbox, read it second). Keep `<script src="nav.js" defer></script>` before `</body>`.
- Title: "Snow 32 Sandbox". Links back to `vfx-snow-32px-topdown-pixel.html`, to the original `vfx-snow-sandbox.html` ("the same engine at 8 px tiles") and to `index.html`.

## World
- A top-down 3/4 winter village corner at 640x360: a grid of 32x32 px tiles, 20 wide by 11.25 tall (the last row is partial). Treat one tile as one Tibia square.
- Content, all drawn in code with care (this is the art half of the study): a log house about 4x3 tiles with a pitched roof, a door and lit windows; 6 to 8 pines about 1.5 tiles wide and 2 to 2.5 tiles tall; a wooden fence; a few rocks; a lamp post; a dirt path 1 tile wide; a frozen pond about 4x3 tiles; a well or a cart as a prop. Ground tiles are grass with variation picked by a position hash, so no grid shows.
- Art rules: a limited palette with ramps (cold blue-violet shadows, warm lights), 1 px dark outlines on sprites and objects, clusters instead of single stray pixels, no gradients. Objects have a drawn summer look and the snow sits on them by rule.
- Actors, sprites built in code:
  - the player, 24x32 px (about 20 to 24 px of drawn width inside the frame), 4 directions, a walk cycle of 4 frames per direction with two foot-contact frames, an idle frame;
  - a villager on a waypoint loop, same size, different palette;
  - a fox about 20x12 px that crosses now and then;
  - a "crowd" slider of 0 to 20 other players who walk tile to tile on random errands (the MMO case: many trails).
- Movement: free 8-way movement for the player by default (arrows / WASD, or tap to walk). A toggle "tile steps" makes every actor walk tile to tile as in Tibia (one tile per 400 ms at full speed, illustrative).

## Layers (must match report §7, bottom to top)
1. **Server weather and tile snow bands (gameplay truth).** Weather scalars (intensity, temperature, wind bucket, sun, time, seed) and one snow band 0..3 per 32 px tile (20 x 12 = 240 tiles), derived from the mean field depth of the tile on a slow clock. Bands drive speed and are all the server would store or send. HUD shows bytes (illustrative: weather 8 bytes per change, 2 bits per tile).
2. **Baked maps at field resolution.** Exposure (shelter under eaves and canopies times an upwind ray for wind lee, with a drift band past the lee) and sun shadow. Rebaked only when the wind bucket changes. All lengths are stated in tiles and converted to cells.
3. **Snow field.** Bytes for depth (cap 200), packed and wet. The field resolution is a control: **1:1** (640x360 = 230,400 cells), **1:2** (320x180 = 57,600 cells, the default, the same cell count as VFX #1), **1:4** (160x90 = 14,400 cells). Stochastic ticks as in VFX #1 (deposit, sandpile relax, wind creep, melt, evaporation), with pick counts stated per 1,000 cells so that rates per tile stay equal at every field resolution.
4. **Ground render at art resolution.** For each art pixel: sample the field (nearest or bilinear, a control), compare with a threshold, shade, look up the palette. Threshold styles (a control): 4x4 Bayer plus noise (the VFX #1 look), 8x8 Bayer, and an **art threshold tile** (a 32x32 threshold map built in code to imitate what an artist would paint: snow gathers first in hollows and between grass tufts in clusters, covers tuft tips last). Shading: an edge shadow line on the south and east side of a snow edge, a body colour, a highlight rim on the north and west side, 2 to 3 bands by depth, sparse 1 px sparkles in sun. Packed snow (trails) has its own two colours. Wet ground is darker.
5. **Objects and snow caps.** Roof, canopy, rock top and fence top are field classes with their own caps, as in VFX #1. Snow on them is drawn with a shaped rule (a lip that hangs 1 to 2 px over the south edge of a roof, rounded caps on pine tiers, a line on fence rails), not plain threshold noise.
6. **Characters at 24x32.** Boot-shaped footprints (about 3x5 px each, left and right offset from the centre line, turned to the walking direction) stamped on the walk cycle's contact frames, conserving snow (keep 30%, push the rest into a rim) and marking packed. In snow deeper than a threshold the prints join into a trench about 10 px wide. Sinking: the bottom rows of the sprite are clipped by depth (illustrative: 1 px per 16 units, at most 8 px) and a snow rim is drawn in front of the feet. Slowdown up to 55% from the tile band. Snow settles on hat and shoulders while it snows and the character stands still, and shakes off on walking. Breath puffs below 1 °C. Kicked snow puffs in deep snow. Soft drop shadows on the snow. Everything y-sorted with the objects.
7. **Flakes.** Near layer: z-height flakes of 2 to 3 px (dot, 2x2, small plus) with shadow dots, landing where they fall. Two far layers: cheap hashed 1 to 2 px flakes with parallax and wind sway. Counts stated per tile of screen so they can be compared with VFX #1 (illustrative: near 60, mid 200, far 320 on 640x360). A "flake size" control: 1 px only (the VFX #1 look, to show why it reads too thin), mixed (default), large.
8. **Light and palette.** The world renders to a palette-index buffer. Day and night retint the palette once per frame. Lamp and window light pools pick a warm palette through a threshold. Blue-violet sun shadows on snow.

## Controls
- Weather: heavy snow, light snow, overcast, sunny, auto cycle. Wind slider. Temperature shown.
- Time: speed 1 to 16x, jump to 08:00, jump to 18:30, pause.
- Tool: walk, shovel, add snow. Clear all snow, instant deep snow.
- **Scale (the study's own controls):** field resolution 1:1 / 1:2 / 1:4; field sampling nearest / bilinear; threshold style Bayer 4 / Bayer 8 / art tile; shading flat / banded; flake size 1 px / mixed / large; footprints boot shapes / 2x2 dots (the VFX #1 stamp scaled naively); sinking on / off.
- Crowd slider 0 to 20. Tile steps on / off.
- Views (x-ray): normal, depth, exposure, sun shadow, packed, wet, tile bands (the server's view), field grid (shows the field cells against the art pixels), cost view.
- A button `button[data-demo="vfx1"]` "VFX #1 settings": field 1:1 is not needed; it sets Bayer 4, flat shading, 1 px flakes, 2x2 dot prints, no art threshold, so the reader sees the old look at the new size. A button `button[data-demo="art32"]` restores the 32 px defaults.
- Keyboard: arrows / WASD move, V cycle views, F cycle field resolution, T cycle threshold style, Space pause.

## Efficiency HUD (required, this is half the study)
- Per-layer timing in ms, measured with performance.now() around each layer every frame, smoothed: sim ticks, ground render, objects, characters and prints, flakes, palette and light, total. Frame budget bar against 16.7 ms.
- Counters: field cells and bytes at the current resolution, picks per tick, bytes a Godot texture upload would move per tick (field bytes, dirty-chunk bytes), prints stamped per second, flakes alive, tile-band changes and bytes per second.
- The cost view tints each art pixel by how many times it was written this frame.

## Corrections from the research
From note 11 (pixel-art craft), relayed to the sandbox builder on 12 Nov:
- Layer 4: the art threshold tile guarantees clusters of at least 2x2 px at every level (Derek Yu's "Chunky Pixels Rule"), built from 6 to 10 seed blobs per tile, with dither only in a 2 to 3 px band at the snow edge. Bayer 4 and Bayer 8 stay as comparison styles only. Reason: 4x4 Bayer at level 1/16 puts 64 isolated pixels on a 32 px tile.
- Layer 4: the thinnest cover is a frost-tinted ground colour, so no state depends on a 25 to 50% dither.
- Layer 4 and 8: the snow ramp has 4 to 5 shades with value steps of 10 to 16 points and saturation under 20%. No pure white in the snow body; the top highlight sits near V 93 to 96% (Apollo's brightest colour is `#ebede9`).
- Layer 6: sinking is capped at 6 px when the sprite's head is half its height; 8 px only suits a head of one third. Legs on a 32 px chibi are 6 to 10 px.
- Layer 6: the 4-frame walk has contacts at frames 0 and 2 (Slynyrd). A 6-frame walk with contacts at 0 and 3 is the alternative (Mana Seed).
- Layer 7: default flakes are 1, 2 and 3 px in three ramp colours, far to near. Pure white only on the near layer.

From note 12 (field resolution and Godot), relayed to the sandbox builder on 12 Nov:
- Layer 3 and 4: with bilinear sampling, the threshold swing in art pixels grows with the field ratio. The same per-cell difference is spread over 4 px at 1:4, so a fixed swing makes the dither band 4 times wider than at 1:1. Scale the swing by the ratio so the snow edge keeps the same width at 1:1, 1:2 and 1:4.
- Layer 3: nearest sampling at 1:4 puts 100% of the snow boundary on cell rows (the blocky look). Bilinear at 1:4 measured the same edge statistics as a 1:1 field. Bilinear is the default; nearest stays as the comparison.
- Layer 6: boot prints need a field of 1:2 or finer. At 1:4 a 3x5 px print is smaller than one cell, and left and right prints join into one trench 8 to 12 px wide (the researcher's inference). The sandbox shows this as it is; the HUD or the field control says "prints need 1:2" when 1:4 is selected. Optional if cheap: a fourth field option "split" that keeps depth and wet at 1:4 and the packed (trail) field at 1:2.
- HUD: a Godot Compatibility texture update always uploads the whole image (no sub-rectangle update), so the "upload bytes per tick" counter is the full field size: 921,600 bytes for 640x360 RGBA8, 14,400 bytes for 160x90 R8.

From notes 10, 13 and 14 (games, characters, falling-snow code), relayed to the sandbox builder on 12 Nov:
- Layer 6, stride: 2 boot prints per 32 px tile, 16 px apart, 3 px either side of the centre line, 3x5 px each (the researcher's recommendation, keeping the Pokémon ratio of one footfall per half sprite height). In free movement a print lands every 16 px of travel.
- Layer 6, walk timing: one 4-frame walk cycle per tile. Frame 0 is the left step and stamps the left print at progress 0. Frame 2 is the right step and stamps the right print at progress 0.5. Frames 1 and 3 are passing poses with the body 1 px up. Frame time is the tile time divided by 4. A walk starts on a step pose (Emerald and OTClient do this). Tile time 400 ms sits inside Tibia's 250 to 600 ms.
- Layer 6, sinking: notes 11 and 13 disagree (cap 6 px against bands of 0, 4, 8 and 12 px after Pokémon Platinum, which sinks a 32-unit sprite 12 to 16 units). The lead's decision: sink follows local depth in 2 px steps up to a cap, and the cap is a control with 0 (off), 6 px (default) and 12 px ("deep snow, as in Pokémon Platinum"). This replaces the sinking on / off toggle.
- Layer 6, trench: when the sink hides the boots (4 px or more), stop stamping boots and stamp a bar 10 px wide and 3 px long across the walking direction every 2 px of travel, keeping 40% and pushing the spill sideways only. With rims the trench is 14 to 16 px wide, half a tile. Pokémon Platinum also switches from footprints to a connected trench in deep snow.
- Layer 6, speed: x1.0, x0.85, x0.7 and x0.5 for server tile bands 0 to 3.
- Layer 6, shadow: an ellipse of 14x5 px centred 3 to 4 px above the sprite's bottom edge, drawn by switching snow pixels to the cold shadow shade (no black blend), 2 px smaller per 4 px of sink.
- Layer 6, optional third footprint mode if cheap: "Tibia tile swap". The whole 32 px tile a player steps off shows a drawn footprint overlay for 10 s, then reverts, with no change to the field (Tibia swaps the snow tile to a footprint twin on StepOut and it decays after 10 s; monsters leave none).
- Layer 7, flake coverage: at heavy snow the flakes cover about 1% of the screen pixels, about 2,300 flake pixels on 640x360 (RPG Maker VX Ace 1.27%, MV 0.89%, the VFX #1 sandbox 1.03%).
- Layer 7, flake sizes: "mixed" (default) is far 1 px, mid 2 px, near 3 px. "large" adds near flakes of 6 px, which is 0.1875 of a tile, the ratio RPG Maker VX Ace, MV and Stardew's winter debris all use. "1 px" stays as the VFX #1 comparison.
- Layer 7, fall speed: the near layer falls about 2 tiles per second (64 px/s, Emerald's 1.9 to 2.3). RPG Maker MV's 3.7 tiles per second reads as sleet. Far layers are slower. Whole-pixel positions.
- Layer 7: every game read keeps flakes world-locked. The sandbox has no camera scroll, so nothing changes, but the explainer card says it.

From note 15 (benchmarks), relayed to the sandbox builder on 12 Nov:
- Keep the RNG state in an `Int32Array`, not in a `let` variable. In the bench, a script-level `let rs` made the same sim tick jump from 0.063 ms to 0.21 to 0.24 ms in some JIT states; with the typed array it stayed at 0.065 to 0.067 ms.
- Bake the exposure map with a row sweep (carry the last obstacle and its distance along each row in the wind direction), not with a ray per cell. The bench measured the identical map in 0.49 ms against 15.9 ms at 1:1 (99.7 ms under a 6x CPU throttle).
- Bilinear sampling of the 1:2 field: use the fixed 9/3/3/1 weights (each art pixel takes 9/16 of its own cell and 3/16, 3/16, 1/16 of the three nearest neighbours). The bench measured 0.135 ms for that upsample against 0.594 ms with per-pixel weight tables (the lead first relayed 0.867 ms, which is a whole flat ground pass with table weights, not the upsample alone).
- Reference frame totals at 640x360 on the bench machine (Apple M4 Pro, median): field 1:2 with CPU render 1.36 to 1.44 ms; a naive 1:1 port 2.4 to 2.5 ms. The ground render is 68 to 71% of a CPU frame.

## As built (the final sandbox, 12 Nov). Where this section and the text above disagree, this section is right.
From the builder's two reports. The report's §8 and every figure that mirrors the sandbox follow these numbers.

1. **Server weather and tile bands.** 240 tiles. Band 0 to 3 at mean depth 10 / 40 / 80 units, swept 8 tiles per frame. Speed x1.0 / 0.85 / 0.7 / 0.5. The HUD counts 8 bytes per weather or wind-bucket change, 2 bytes per band change, 60 bytes for the full band set.
2. **Baked maps.** Exposure = shelter times an upwind reach of 3.5 tiles, with a 12% drift band past the lee, rebaked per wind bucket with a row sweep (1.7 ms at 1:1, 0.4 ms at 1:2, 0.1 ms at 1:4 on the builder's machine; 20 to 27 ms at 1:1 before the row sweep). Shelter applies to ground classes only. Sun shadow is baked at art resolution, stored per cell as a coverage byte and drawn from a cached mask. Shadows are solid palette swaps.
3. **Snow field.** 3 bytes per cell (depth cap 200, packed, wet). Modes 1:1 (230,400 cells), 1:2 (57,600, default), 1:4 (14,400) and split (depth and wet at 1:4, packed at 1:2). Picks per 1,000 cells per tick: deposit 15.6, relax 69.4, wind creep 24.3, melt 26, evaporation 27.8 (5.2 when cold). Measured picks per tick in heavy snow: 25,259 / 5,753 / 1,366 at 1:1 / 1:2 / 1:4. Wind creep picks scale by 2 / field ratio so drift speed in px stays equal. Slope limit 1.5 units per art px. Open-cell depth target 110 x exposure x noise (noise period about 1 tile). Ice exposure 0.42. Canopy melt x1.6. Packed fades evenly at 0.22 units per tick (13 per second in heavy snow): a print turns light after 4 s and is gone after 13 s. The VFX #1 rule (14 units of packed removed per snowflake hit) was dropped because it left rubble at this scale.
4. **Ground render.** Nearest or bilinear (default). At 1:2 bilinear uses fixed 9/3/3/1 weights; at 1:4 a 4-entry weight table per axis. Border pixels between surface classes (3% of ground pixels at 1:2, 6.5% at 1:4) read a class-aware weight table so roof and canopy snow never bleeds onto the ground. Thresholds: Bayer 4 plus noise (VFX #1 numbers), Bayer 8, art map. The art map is a 128x128 px tileable map (not one 32x32 tile, so the period does not show), about 6 seed blobs per 32 px tile, stored in 2x2 px blocks, range about 4 to 55 units. The dither swing is scaled by 2 / field ratio (the whole Bayer swing, and the edge-dither term of the art map). Coverage test is depth + packed/2. Frost tint on bare ground from depth 4. Banded shading: a 1 px highlight rim on north and west edges, a shadow line on south and east edges (2 px on the south once the snow is 14 units over its threshold), one body colour, 1 or 2 short drift lines per tile from depth 34 (each a 1 px highlight over a 1 px shadow), one arc of 12 to 22 px on about 4 tiles in 10 from depth 62, a lit or shaded side for slopes over 32 units across 6 px, 1 px sparkles in sun. Slope shading is off within 3 px of packed snow. "Flat" is the VFX #1 emboss.
5. **Objects and caps.** 23,877 cap pixels (10% of the screen) are tested per frame; object art is static and cached. The roof fills from the ridge in scallops and ends in a lip 2 rows over the eave with a 3 px thickness band; a covered roof has a 2 px ridge highlight and a north slope in the light shadow tone. Pine caps follow each tier's visible top, up to about 60% of the tier. Fence rail line and post caps; rock, well, chimney, lamp and window-sill caps. The prop is a well (no cart).
6. **Characters.** 24x32 px frames, drawn about 20 px wide and 30 px tall, 7 palettes, 3 directions drawn plus a flip. Walk = 4 frames, 8 px per frame (100 ms at 80 px/s, 400 ms per tile), steps on frames 0 and 2, body 1 px up on frames 1 and 3, a walk starts on a step pose. On a step frame the swinging boot lifts 2 px and the opposite arm drops 2 px. Boot print 4x6 px (6x4 px sideways), which is 2x3 whole cells at 1:2, left and right rows 6 px apart (2 px of snow between them), one print per foot every 16 px of travel, keep 30%, spill to an even ring, packed 255. A print draws as a hole: a 1 px dark line on its north and west inner edge (`#575b76`), a floor of `#7b8199`, a 1 px highlight on the far rim. At 1:1 the print keeps a heel gap. At 1:4 one row keeps blunt marks and the other shrinks to slivers (prints do not join into a trail). Sink in 2 px steps, cap 0 / 6 / 12 px, full cap at 120 units (cap 6 = 2 px per 40 units). Trench from 4 px of sink: a round brush of radius 5 px (10 px across) every 2 px of travel, floor = 40% of ambient depth, spill to both sides along the perpendicular; channel floor `#a6b1c0` with a 2 px shadow on the north and west wall and a 1 px highlight on the far rim. A 3-row snow rim in front of the legs. Shadow 14x5 px, 3 px above the feet, a palette swap, 2 px smaller per 4 px of sink. A 1 px line of snow on hat and shoulders at 0.3 of a 9 s idle timer and a second row on both at 0.65 (read from `drawChar`; the builder's report said hat first, then shoulders). The trench marks cells packed 160 and spreads its spill over 10 deposits beside the walls; the sink reads the snow at 4 points 6 to 9 px to the sides and skips packed cells (`ambient()`). Breath below 1 °C. Fox 20x12 px, 2 frames. Crowd 0 to 20 with BFS errands, no collision between actors. Footprint modes: boot prints, 2x2 dots (the VFX #1 stamp every 4 px), Tibia tile swap (a drawn overlay for 10 s, no field change, the fox leaves none).
7. **Flakes.** Far 560 x 1 px, mid 300 x 2 px, near 150 x 3 px plus shapes (10% are 4 px). Measured 1,010 alive, about 2,100 to 2,200 px, 0.9 to 0.97% of the screen. Fall 26 / 42 / 64 px/s (the near layer is 2 tiles per second). The near layer has height, a shadow dot and deposits 2 units on landing. "Large" adds 6 px near flakes. "1 px" mode covers 0.39%.
8. **Light and palette.** 77 base colours x 6 versions (plain, sun shadow, wet, wet shadow, frost, frost shadow) = 462 entries, plus 2 warm palettes. A Uint16 index buffer. Lamp and two window pools in 2 steps with a Bayer 8 band. Snow ramp `#f2f1ec`, `#cbd3dc` (body), `#9eabb8`, `#7d7a96`, `#5a5674`.
