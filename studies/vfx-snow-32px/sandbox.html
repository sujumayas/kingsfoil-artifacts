Walk with the arrows or WASD, or tap the ground. Switch the tool to Shovel or Add snow and drag on the scene. Keys: V next view, F next field resolution, T next threshold style, Space pause. The canvas is 640 by 360 px, which is 20 by 11.25 tiles of 32 px. The snow engine is the one from VFX #1. Only the scale changed.

1 · Server weather and tile bands

The server owns the weather: intensity, temperature, wind bucket, sun, time and a seed. That is 8 bytes per change. It also keeps one snow band per 32 px tile, 0 to 3, taken from the mean depth of the tile on a slow clock. 240 tiles at 2 bits is 60 bytes for the whole screen. The band sets walking speed: 100%, 85%, 70% and 50%. Everything else on this page is drawn by the client. Tile bands view shows what the server knows.

2 · Baked maps at field resolution

Exposure and sun shadow are baked bytes, one per field cell. Exposure is shelter under eaves and canopies, times a look upwind for 3.5 tiles. A tall object puts the cell in its lee. Just past the lee is a 12% drift band. The bake is one sweep along each row in the wind direction that carries the last obstacle and its distance, so it visits each cell once. It runs again only when the wind crosses a bucket. All lengths are in tiles, so the reach is 28 cells at 1:4 and 112 at 1:1. The HUD shows the last bake time.

3 · Snow field

Three bytes per cell: depth (cap 200), packed and wet. The field can be 640 by 360 (1:1), 320 by 180 (1:2, the VFX #1 cell count) or 160 by 90 (1:4). Each tick picks random cells: 15.6 per 1,000 cells for snowfall, 69.4 for the sandpile pass, 24.3 for wind creep, 26 for melt and 27.8 for evaporation. The rates per cell are the same at every resolution, so a tile fills at the same speed. Wind creep takes twice the picks at 1:1 and half at 1:4, so a drift moves the same pixels per second. The slope limit is 1.5 depth units per art pixel. Split keeps depth and wet at 1:4 and only the packed trail field at 1:2.

4 · Ground render at art resolution

Each of the 230,400 art pixels samples the field, compares it with a threshold and picks a palette index. Bilinear sampling at 1:2 uses fixed weights: 9/16 of the pixel's own cell and 3/16, 3/16 and 1/16 of the three nearest. At 1:4 the weights come from a 4-entry table per axis. Where two classes meet (ground, roof, canopy, rock top, fence top), 3% of the ground pixels at 1:2, a baked weight table mixes only cells of the pixel's own class, so roof snow never bleeds onto the ground. Bayer 4 and Bayer 8 are the old ordered dither. The art tile is a 128 px tileable threshold map with about 6 seed blobs per 32 px tile, stored in 2 by 2 px blocks, so snow arrives as patches that grow and merge and never as single pixels. From 4 units of depth the bare ground takes a frost tint. The dither swing is scaled by 2 over the field ratio, so an edge is as wide at 1:4 as at 1:1. Banded shading draws a 1 px light rim on north and west edges and a shadow line on south and east edges, 2 px on the south side once the snow is 14 units over its threshold. The body is one colour at any time of day. Settled snow over 34 units shows 1 or 2 short drawn drift lines per tile, each a 1 px highlight with a 1 px shadow under it. Over 62 units a longer arc of 12 to 22 px shows on about 4 tiles in 10. A slope of more than 32 units across 6 px, with no packed snow beside it, gets a lit or a shaded side. That is how an old trench or a shovelled edge still reads after its packed mark is gone.

5 · Objects and snow caps

Roofs, pine tiers, rock tops and fence tops are field classes with their own cells. Each object carries a drawn threshold map that says in which order snow covers it. The roof fills from the ridge down in scallops and ends in a lip that hangs 2 px over the eave. A covered roof keeps a 2 px highlight along the ridge and a shaded north slope. Each pine tier gets a cap that follows its upper edge and leaves the dark underside free. Rails get a line and posts get a cap. The object art is static and cached. Only the 23,877 cap pixels are tested each frame, about 10% of the screen.

6 · Characters at 24 by 32

The walk cycle has 4 frames per tile. Frames 0 and 2 are the steps. On a step the planted boot is drawn one row taller, the swinging boot lifts 2 px, the opposite arm swings 2 px and the body leans 1 px over the planted foot. Frames 1 and 3 raise the body 1 px. Each step stamps a boot print of 4 by 6 px, or 6 by 4 px when walking sideways. That is 2 by 3 cells of the 1:2 field. Left and right prints sit in two rows 6 px apart, so 2 px of snow stay between them, and a print lands every 16 px. A print keeps 30% of the depth, pushes the rest into a rim and sets the packed field to 255. It is drawn as a hole: a dark line on its north and west inner edge, a floor two ramp steps under the snow body, and a 1 px highlight on the far rim. Snowfall fades the packed field by 13 units per second, so a print turns light after 4 s and is gone after 13 s, and a trail fades from its old end. Sinking clips the legs in 2 px steps. Snow 120 units deep sinks a character by the whole cap you choose, so the 6 px cap gives 2 px per 40 units. From 4 px of sink the boots are hidden, so the character ploughs a channel with a round brush 10 px across and keeps 40% of the depth in it. The spill goes to both sides. The channel has a 2 px shadow on its sun-side wall and a highlight on the far rim. A snow rim is drawn in front of the legs. Snow settles on hats and shoulders while a character stands still. 2x2 dots is the VFX #1 stamp at its old size. Tibia tile swap changes no field at all. The tile a player leaves shows drawn prints for 10 s, and the fox leaves none.

7 · Flakes

Three layers, all locked to the world and drawn on whole pixels. The near layer has real height. Its flakes are 3 px plus shapes, one in ten is 4 px, in pure white. They fall 2 tiles per second, cast a shadow dot and add depth where they land. The mid and far layers are hashed positions with no state, 2 px and 1 px, slower and with wind sway. Their colours are two steps of a blue-grey ramp. At heavy snow the three layers hold 1,010 flakes and cover about 0.9% of the screen, close to what RPG Maker and the VFX #1 sandbox draw. The HUD shows the live count. 1 px keeps the old flake size and reads thin at this scale. Large adds 6 px flakes to the near layer.

8 · Light and palette

The world is drawn into a palette index buffer. Day and night retint the palette once per frame, not the 230,400 pixels. Each colour has six versions: plain, sun shadow, wet, wet in shadow, frost and frost in shadow. The lamp and the two windows pick a warm palette where a baked light map passes a threshold, in two steps with a narrow dither band between them. Shadows on snow switch to a cold shade. Nothing is blended with black.

What it costs

Every layer is timed with performance.now() each frame and smoothed. The ground render is the fixed cost. It touches every art pixel at any field size. The field size sets the sim cost and the upload size. Godot's Compatibility renderer uploads a whole texture on every change, so the upload per tick is the full field. That is 230,400 bytes per channel at 1:1 and 14,400 at 1:4. The HUD also shows what a sub-rectangle update of dirty 32 px chunks would move, if Godot had one. Cost view counts how many times each pixel was written this frame. Field grid draws the field cells under the art.

What changed from VFX #1

The canvas has 4 times the pixels and a tile has 16 times. A 1 px flake, a 2 by 2 px footprint and a Bayer dusting were sized for 8 px tiles. Press VFX #1 settings to see them at this scale, then 32 px defaults. The depth field did not have to grow. At 1:2 with bilinear sampling it draws the same edges as 1:1. Boot prints are the one thing that needs 1:2. At 1:4 a cell is 4 px wide, so a 4 by 6 px print covers its cells unevenly. One row keeps blunt marks, the other shrinks to slivers, and the pair stops reading as two feet. Split keeps the prints by giving only the packed field the 1:2 grid.
