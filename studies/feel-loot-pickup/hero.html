Loot and pickup in a top-down pixel MMORPG: drops, reveals, pickup, sharing and death

A Tibia monster dies into a corpse that already holds its loot, and a chat line says what dropped. Nothing pops, glows or flies, and players click corpses so fast that any animation feels like lag. The ARPGs throw items out of the body on a 400 to 650 ms arc, ring them on landing and stand a beam over the rare ones. Both break when 30 players share a screen. This study follows the item from the corpse into the bag. It covers containers and spills, the drop arc, five rarity tiers, labels on demand, quick loot, magnets, who owns a drop and for how long, races and prediction over a server, and what a player loses on death. We measured what loot costs to draw. Items are cheap; blurred glows and per-glyph labels are not. Aimed at Godot 4 on the web and a server-authoritative MMO.
