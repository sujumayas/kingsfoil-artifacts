Agent 2, Curves #3 (Asterion). Written 2026-11-13. Angle: how the modern games refined the classic death penalty,

## Q2. How each softened or hardened the death penalty

Takeaway. Every modern twin that kept item loss added a fee, a timer or a grade so the loss could be bought back or
chosen; every twin that kept exp loss carved out a no-loss band at low level; nobody added a harder flat penalty after
launch and kept it. The trade is consistent: fear is kept where the player opted in (zone colour, skull, endgame map),
churn is cut where the death was not chosen (low level, repeat death, unflagged zone).

Cited findings.
- OSRS, ground items then gravestone. 24 Oct 2013: dropped items invisible to others for 3 min, then visible to the
  owner 1 more min, then public. 2014: items stay 30 min. 22 May 2015: 60 min. 25 June 2020: gravestone plus Death's
  Office replace ground items; grave lasts 15 min; items under 100,000 gp reclaim free, above that a 5 % fee; grave
  reclaim is tiered and capped at 1 % at worst; ironmen pay half; the office holds 120 stacks. [V, E3] [1][2]
  Reading: Jagex moved from "items on the floor for a timer" to "items safe, pay a fee" in three steps over seven years,
  each step polled. The 2013 three-minute window is almost exactly Asterion's bones window in spirit (your own loot,
  briefly protected).
- RS3. Death's office is the parent system; the RS3 wiki NPC page gives Death's debut 31 Oct 2005 and the office's
  graphical rework 26 Oct 2020 but not the office's launch date or fee formula on the fetched page. [V, E3] [3] The
  2015 date in the brief is [U].
- Albion Online. Four zone colours. Yellow: a flagged PvP death is a knockdown of about 3 min with 5 % durability
  loss, no loot drop. Red and black: full loot; a community guide lists about 30 % of dropped items trashed (destroyed)
  and 20 to 40 % durability loss on the rest; silver is never taken from inventory, the killer's silver is generated
  from the durability lost. Blue: knockdown, sources disagree on durability. [V, E3 with E4 numbers] [4][5][6]
  Official wiki blocked, so the 30 % trash figure is community-reported.
- Valheim. Default: lose 5 % of every skill level and the progress toward the next level; a "No Skill Drain" status
  for 10 min (600 s check) after a death so a second death on the corpse run costs nothing; tombstone holds the
  inventory. World modifiers (Hildir's Request update, 2023) make death penalty a server setting: Casual, Very Easy,
  Easy, Normal, Hard, Hardcore (character deleted). Community hosts list the skill loss as 0.75 / 0.75 / 2.5 / 5 /
  7.5 / 100 %; other hosts say Casual is 0 %. [V, E3 conflicting] [7][8][9]
- Project Gorgon. Death has no exp loss and no item loss. Dying is a skill (max 50, 78,010 exp to cap) that pays
  bonus exp for each new cause of death; a cosmetic tombstone; an optional one-hour role-play effect (phobia,
  "Manic Laughter"); diseases can be permanent until cured; Hardcore Mode (opt in at a signpost in Serbule) breaks items
  until you walk back to your tombstone. [V, E3] [10]
- Path of Exile 2 (EA Dec 2024). No exp loss during the campaign (normal and cruel acts); 10 % of the current level's
  exp bar on death in endgame maps; players report it binding from about level 71 to 100. GGG's public line in press:
  the loss is a challenge, not a punishment, and the design "isn't for everyone". Forum threads with 20+ pages of
  debate since launch. [V, E3/E2 via press] [11][12][13] PoE 1 specifics (5 % in acts 6 to 10, 10 % in maps, level 100 at
  4,250,334,444 exp) are from memory and tagged [U]; one old patch note quoted in a thread shows the Merciless
  penalty cut from 20 % to 15 % in the pre-3.0 era, so the penalty has only ever been lowered. [V, E4] [12]
- Dark Souls to Elden Ring. All runes drop at the spot of death; die again before picking them up and they are gone.
  A compass marker points to them. Sacrificial Twig (consumed instead of the runes) and the Twiggy Cracked Tear
  (temporary) are insurance. The wiki calls runes analogous to Souls. [V, E3] [14]
- WoW. Beta had a 50 % exp cut after some hours of play; testers hated it; Blizzard flipped it into rested exp
  (200 % for a while) and called it the same numbers from the other side. Patch 1.1.0 (7 Nov 2004) replaced exp loss
  with resurrection sickness at 100 % durability loss; 1.1.1 (17 Nov 2004) capped it at 10 min and 25 % durability;
  10.0.0 (25 Oct 2022) cut the duration to 1 min and raised durability loss to 50 %. Sickness is −75 % to base stats
  and damage, not applied under level 10. [V, E3] [15][16]
- FFXI. No exp loss at level 30 or below or inside cities; at level 99 about 625 exp lost per KO; a Raise spell
  restores part; level down is possible. [V, E3] [17] The 10 % capped at 2400 rule is [U].
- Haven & Hearth. Death is permanent for the character; a new character inherits 15 % of spent LP and FEP with no
  burial, 25 % with the skull, 35 % with the body, 45 % with the whole body or a state funeral; lamenting adds 6 % of
  attributes per prayer up to 45 %; inheritance unlocks 8 h after the ancestor's birth; knock-outs are a separate
  state with a one-minute get-up. [V, E3] [18]
- EVE. Pod death destroys the implants in that clone and respawns you at the home station; jump clones unaffected;
  clone contracts cost 900,000 ISK. [V, E3] [19] The removal of skill point loss with clone grades (Rhea, Dec 2014) is
  [U]; the fetched page does not cover it.
- Ashes of Creation (alpha 2, from Oct 2024). Experience debt that can span multiple levels, stat dampening while in
  debt, reduced drop rates, gear durability loss on death, corruption for PKs. No official percentages reachable. The
  design lead's stated intent is for death to be punishing. [V, E4] [20][21]
- Black Desert. Death penalties (crystal destruction, exp loss) were removed for world bosses, field bosses and
  Atoraxxion monsters on normal servers, and in a later PvP patch players with karma ≥ 0 stopped losing exp when killed
  in PvP; gankers still lose. Dates not on the fetched snippets. [V, E3] [22][23]

Inferences.
- The lineage runs: full drop on the floor (2002 era) -> timed visibility (OSRS 2013) -> long timer (OSRS 2015) ->
  safe grave with fee (RS3, OSRS 2020) -> no item loss, durability only (WoW) -> no loss at all (Project Gorgon).
  Asterion's 30-minute lootable corpse sits at the 2014 OSRS point.
- Fear is preserved in every modern design by making the big loss opt in. Albion by zone colour, OSRS by Wilderness
  and skull, PoE by endgame maps, Valheim by server setting, Project Gorgon by a Hardcore signpost. Asterion's current
  design has one fear level for everyone and everywhere; Gorothland versus Akron is the free knob.
- Repeat death is the churn point the moderns fixed first (Valheim grace, Souls single chance, OSRS 15 min safe
  grave). Asterion has the Heartstone destroyed by death, which makes the second trip slower, not safer.

Gaps. Official Albion durability and trash percentages; RS3 Death's office launch date and fee formula; PoE 1 exact
tiers and the 2024/25 developer interviews (only press paraphrase found); EVE skill-loss removal date; Black Desert
patch dates; anything on Pax Dei.

## Q3. Skill by use in modern design

Takeaway. The moderns keep "you are what you practise" but bolt on three fixes the classics lacked: a per-node
curve that resets per weapon (Albion), a hard total cap that forces choice (Mortal Online 2, ~1100 to 1200 points), and
a repeat-death floor so the practised skill is not the thing death takes (Valheim's 5 % and grace timer). Project
Gorgon shows that with no classes and no death cost, the skill list itself becomes the content.

Cited findings.
- Albion destiny board. Each equipment line has a mastery node and an individual item node; fame earned while
  wearing the item goes to those nodes; masteries go to 100 and elite specialisations to 120; once both nodes are at
  100 the extra fame becomes Fame Credits spendable on any combat node you have a level in; Auto-Respec quadruples
  credit gain at 1 silver per credit; Learning Points buy levels and are worth more fame at higher levels. [V, E3]
  [24][25][26] The per-level fame table was not reachable.
- Mortal Online 2. Primary skills share a common cap, about 1100 skill points plus attribute bonuses to around 1200,
  so no character can train everything; 2.0.6 (July 2024) added 1000 Mastery levels and 110 Mastery Points over 440+
  gifts, with Action Point Pool and Profession Point Pool among the gifts. Store text claims 600+ skills. [V, E3/E4]
  [27][28][29]
- Valheim. Skills go to 100 and gain by use; death takes 5 % of each level and the partial progress; the rested
  effect raises skill gain (fandom page blocked, so the exact multiplier is [U]). [V, E3] [7][8]
- Project Gorgon. Dozens of skills, each a level curve with synergy levels between skills (Dying gives +1 Necromancy
  at 20 and 30, +1 Vampirism at 50). Levels follow a table, Dying 1 = 10 exp, Dying 50 = 78,010 exp. [V, E3] [10]
- Morrowind, Oblivion, Skyrim. UESP blocked (403), so this is [U]: Oblivion levels the character after 10 major
  skill increases and grants attribute bonuses up to +5 per attribute from 10 governed-skill increases, while enemies
  scale with level, so unplanned practice produces a weaker character than a planned one ("efficient leveling");
  Skyrim removed attributes and let every skill increase feed one character level, removing the planning tax. [U]
  [30][31]

Inferences.
- Albion's fame credits are the modern answer to Tibia's "a knight who tries axe late is doomed": practice is still
  the input, but surplus practice becomes a currency you can redirect. Asterion has four skills and no vocation, so the
  problem is smaller; the Albion knob is relevant only if more weapon skills are added.
- Mortal Online 2's cap is the opposite answer: force identity by scarcity. With four skills Asterion needs no cap,
  but the Tibia-style geometric tries curve already acts as a soft cap in hours (sword 100 in about 707 h).
- The efficient-leveling failure is the risk for any by-use system where level drives difficulty. Asterion ties
  creature difficulty to geography, not to player level, so it is immune by construction. Worth saying in the report.

Gaps. Albion fame per mastery level; Valheim rested skill multiplier; UESP figures for Oblivion and Skyrim.

## Q4. The long curve and its dead stretches

Takeaway. The moderns pace months by stacking many short curves rather than one long one: Albion has a 100 (or 120)
level curve per item node, OSRS has 23 skills at 99 each, PoE resets every 3 to 4 months by league, Valheim gates by
biome boss. Each gives a milestone every few hours even when the headline number barely moves.

Cited findings.
- OSRS max (all 99s). Records of 771 h (He Box Jonge) and 791 h (JCW); realistic estimates 2000 to 2500 h, casual
  3700 to 4000 h; single skills from about 40 h (Fletching) to 300+ h (Runecraft, Agility). [V, E3/E4, commercial
  sites] [32][33] The exp table is the same cubic family as Tibia's: level 99 is 13,034,431 exp with each level about
  1.1 times the last [U].
- Albion. Masteries to 100, specialisations to 120, then fame credits; premium status is reported to double fame per
  hour (low-quality source, [U]). Fame is earned per hour hunting, so the curve is in hours played, not days. [V, E3]
  [24][25]
- PoE. Level 100 is reached by very few; the 10 % penalty at 90+ makes the last 10 levels the dead stretch the
  community argues about; leagues reset the economy every few months so most characters never reach it. [V, E3]
  [11][12]
- Project Gorgon. Skills cap at 50 to 100 depending on the skill; the population curve (443 average in July 2026
  to 251 in the last 30 days, all-time peak 4,114 in Feb 2026) is the honest measure of how a long no-class curve
  retains a small audience. [V, E1] [34]
- Valheim. Ten-ish biomes, each with a boss that unlocks the next; skill curve to 100 by use; a standard co-op
  playthrough is tens of hours, not months. [V, E3] [7]

Inferences.
- Asterion's three curves (cubic exp, geometric skills, geometric magic level) share one axis of hours but their
  milestones are not interleaved on purpose. Albion's trick is to make every hunt advance a node that is near a level,
  so a 45-minute session almost always ends on a ding. Cheap to copy: show skill progress to the next rung and tune
  the sword base so a rung lands roughly every session until 50.
- The known dead stretches in the twins are all at the top (PoE 95 to 100, OSRS 92 to 99 where half the exp lives,
  Albion 100 to 120). Asterion's Gorothland band (100+) will be the same place; plan for horizontal content there,
  not a steeper curve.

Gaps. A fame-per-level table; OSRS exp table cited directly; PoE level-100 counts per league.

## Q5. Session friendliness

Takeaway. The two designs that most reward being away are WoW's rested exp (200 % after time away, cap 1.5 levels)
and EVE's offline skill queue; both come from a studio deciding the alternative (penalising long play) was hated in
beta. Albion added a mobile client and short dungeon formats; OSRS kept slow and offers afk training instead.

Cited findings.
- WoW rested. 200 % exp while rested on kills and gathering (not quests); accrues 5 % of a level per 8 h in an inn or
  city (one bubble per 16 h), a quarter of that elsewhere; cap 150 % of a level, 160 h to fill from zero; grew out of a
  beta fatigue system that cut exp 50 % after a few hours and was rejected by testers. [V, E2/E3] [15]
- Valheim. Rested buff from sitting by a fire under a roof speeds regen and skill gain; co-op worlds are hosted by one
  friend or a dedicated server, so the group plays when the host is up. World modifiers include Portals and Raids
  settings that remove travel and interruption friction. [V, E3] [8]
- OSRS. The 2020 Death's office and 15-minute grave mean a death no longer ends a session; communities asked for and
  polled every change (the wiki's history lists reverts in Nov 2013 and Sept and Oct 2014 when changes were disliked).
  [V, E3] [1]
- PoE 2. The debate is explicitly about session shape: a 10 % loss at level 90+ erases hours, so players stop
  mapping late at night. [V, E3] [11][13]
- Project Gorgon. "Enter the light" at Dying 10 gives a free teleport to a hub, with Serbule and Rahu portals on an 8
  h cooldown, which makes death a fast-travel tool. [V, E3] [10]

Inferences.
- Asterion's anti-compulsion stance forbids daily rewards but rested exp is not a daily reward: it pays for absence,
  never punishes it, and WoW's own framing shows the same numbers can be read either way. A rested multiplier (for
  example 1.5× exp and skill tries for the first 30 min after 8 h away, capped) fits the 30 to 60 minute session
  target and costs one timestamp per character.
- When the slow version is kept (OSRS, PoE), the community accepts it only when the choice is visible and polled or
  when the penalty is opt in by zone or endgame. A flat penalty with no choice is what PoE 2 is being criticised for.

Gaps. Valheim rested multiplier; Albion mobile client date and session telemetry (vendor); any published session
length data for OSRS.

## Q6. Small-group and private-server practice

Takeaway. Where players run their own servers, the death penalty becomes a setting and the exp rate becomes a
multiplier; the defaults stay close to the retail rule and groups pick softer. Valheim ships this as six named
presets; the RSPS scene ships exp multipliers; Project Gorgon shows the retail-server floor for a tiny population.

Cited findings.
- Valheim world modifiers: death penalty Casual to Hardcore, resources, raids, portals, combat; hosts document that
  the server setting overrides the client's; Hardcore deletes the character. [V, E3] [8][9]
- OSRS private servers: one search returned no documentation of typical rates (x5 to x50 is folklore, [U]); the
  retail record of 771 h to max is the baseline any multiplier divides. [U] [32]
- Project Gorgon population: 251 average players in the last 30 days (Nov 2026), 24 h peak 290, all-time peak
  4,114. A no-class skill-by-use MMO sustains about 250 concurrent on a single shard with no death penalty. [V, E1]
  [34]
- Albion has no private servers; Mortal Online 2 has none. Haven & Hearth runs on one official world with resets
  ("worlds" numbered), and inheritance is the within-world reset. [V, E3] [18]

Inferences.
- Asterion already stores `deathExpLoss` and `gearDropChance` as constants; exposing them as server config with two
  or three named presets (Valheim's model) costs almost nothing and lets the 5 friends run the natural experiment
  themselves. The telemetry (death events, session events) is already there to read the result.

Gaps. RSPS exp-rate documentation; Albion or Valheim dedicated-server skill-rate settings beyond the death penalty.

## Pattern cards

### Graded loss by zone
- Lineage: Ultima Online Trammel/Felucca split (2000) -> Albion zone colours (2017) -> OSRS Wilderness and skull.
- Feeling: Challenge where chosen, Fellowship and Discovery where not. The player names the stakes.
- Mechanism: a per-zone table {knockdown or death, durability %, trash %, inventory drop yes/no}. Albion yellow:
  knockdown 3 min, 5 % durability; red/black: full loot, about 30 % trashed.
- Works when zones are legible and the reward scales with the colour. Breaks when the safe zone has the best loot.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [4][5][6].
- Cost S. Knobs: per-world drop chance, bag drop on/off, corpse timer per world.
- Asterion: Akron (1 to 35) could drop only the bag with 0 % gear chance, Ingwen the current rule, Gorothland the
  current rule plus a longer corpse. Geography already is the ladder (D147), so this is a column on an existing table.

### Reclaim fee (safe grave)
- Lineage: RS3 gravestones (2007) -> RS3 Death's office -> OSRS Death's office (25 June 2020).
- Feeling: Submission (routine) with a small Challenge sting; converts fear into a gold sink.
- Mechanism: items go to a grave for 15 min; reclaim free under a value threshold, else a percentage fee (5 %,
  tiered to 1 % at the grave).
- Works in an economy with gold sinks. Breaks when fees are trivial for veterans and ruinous for new players unless
  tiered.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [1][2].
- Cost M (new NPC, pricing table). Knobs: threshold, fee %, grave minutes.
- Asterion: the temple already heals for free; a temple reclaim for the backpack at a fee (not the gear slots) keeps
  the 10 % per slot fear and removes the "lost the whole bag to a passer-by" churn.

### Recoverable single-chance drop plus insurance consumable
- Lineage: Demon's Souls (2009) -> Dark Souls (2011) -> Elden Ring (2022, Sacrificial Twig).
- Feeling: Challenge, fiero on the pick-up.
- Mechanism: the loss is placed in the world once; a second death before pick-up deletes it; a consumable is spent
  instead of the loss.
- Works with a visible marker and a short way back. Breaks when the way back is the thing that killed you and no
  alternative route exists.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [14].
- Cost S. Knobs: whether others can loot, marker on/off, insurance price.
- Asterion: the corpse is already this; adding a map or compass marker to your own corpse and a buyable "bendición"
  that saves the backpack once is the Souls refinement. Blessings exist in Tibia 8+ (agent 8), so cite both lineages.

### Grace timer on repeat death
- Lineage: Valheim No Skill Drain (10 min).
- Feeling: fairness; keeps Challenge without the spiral.
- Mechanism: `if now - lastDeath < 600 s then skillLoss = 0`.
- Works always; breaks only if abused to suicide-teleport, which Valheim allows anyway.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [7][9].
- Cost S (one timestamp). Knob: window length, what it covers (exp, gear, bag).
- Asterion: no second exp loss within 15 min of a death. The corpse run would then be a risk to the body you are
  fetching, not to the level you just lost.

### Death penalty as a server preset
- Lineage: Valheim world modifiers (2023) -> hosting guides.
- Feeling: autonomy for the group (player-steered difficulty, not DDA).
- Mechanism: named presets mapping to `deathExpLoss`, `gearDropChance`, corpse minutes.
- Works for a friends server; breaks on a public shard where presets split the population.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [8][9].
- Cost S. Knobs: the three constants.
- Asterion: expose the existing constants in server config with "clásico / templado / duro" presets.

### Exp debt instead of exp loss
- Lineage: Dark Age of Camelot and City of Heroes (debt) [U] -> Ashes of Creation alpha 2.
- Feeling: the loss is felt as slower progress, not as a number going down.
- Mechanism: on death add debt = X % of a level; half of future exp pays the debt until clear; optional stat
  dampening while in debt.
- Works for players who hate seeing the bar move backward. Breaks if debt caps are high and stack (Ashes forum
  complaints about multi-level debt).
- Evidence: [V, E4] [20][21].
- Cost M. Knobs: debt %, repayment share, cap.
- Asterion: a variant that keeps the "one session of progress" calibration while removing level-down; worth an A/B on
  the friends server because the vision doc wants death to matter visibly.

### Rested exp
- Lineage: WoW beta fatigue (−50 %) inverted into rested (+100 %), 2004.
- Feeling: Submission; the game rewards coming back.
- Mechanism: accrue 5 % of a level per 8 h away (capped at 150 %); consume at 2× while hunting.
- Works for short-session adults. Breaks if it feels like a login reward (framing).
- Evidence: [V, E2/E3] [15].
- Cost S. Knobs: accrual rate, cap, which gains it covers (exp only, or skill tries too).
- Asterion: fits "progress waits for you" if framed as rest, not reward.

### Inheritance on permadeath
- Lineage: Haven & Hearth (15 to 45 % carried over by burial quality).
- Feeling: Narrative and Fellowship (funerals are social).
- Mechanism: a table of carry-over percentages keyed to what the group recovers from the body.
- Works in a village game. Breaks in a solo game.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [18].
- Cost L (new character flow). Not for Asterion now; note it as the hard end of the lineage.

### No-loss band at low level
- Lineage: FFXI (no exp loss at or under 30) -> PoE (no loss in campaign) -> WoW (no sickness under 10).
- Feeling: onboarding safety without a tutorial.
- Mechanism: `if level <= N then expLoss = 0`.
- Evidence: [V, E3] [17][11][16].
- Cost S. Knob: N.
- Asterion: the calibration test shows a death at level ≤ 4 costs about 10 min already; a band of 0 % to level 8
  (end of the censo chain) would protect the first evening.

## Glossary additions
- Corpse run: the trip from respawn back to the body to recover what dropped. Source: Valheim tombstone, OSRS grave
  [7][1].
- Knockdown: a non-lethal defeat state with a timer and a small durability cost, used instead of death in safer zones.
  Source: Albion yellow zones [4].
- Graded loss: a death penalty whose size is set by the zone or mode the player chose to enter. Source: Albion [4].
- Reclaim fee: gold paid to recover dropped items from a safe holding place. Source: OSRS Death's office [2].
- Grace timer: a window after a death during which a second death costs nothing. Source: Valheim No Skill Drain [9].
- Exp debt: experience owed and repaid from future gains instead of removed from the bar. Source: Ashes forums [20].
- Rested exp: a multiplier on exp earned after time away, with a cap. Source: WoW [15].
- Inheritance: the share of a dead character's progress carried to a new one. Source: Haven & Hearth [18].
- World modifier: a server-side preset changing death penalty, resources or raids for one world. Source: Valheim [8].
- Fame credit: surplus practice converted into a currency spendable on other skill nodes. Source: Albion [24].
- Delevel: losing enough exp to drop a level. Source: FFXI [17].
