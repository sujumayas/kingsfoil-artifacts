Enemy AI for a Hero Defense RTSFrom if/else to decision models

Kingsfoil Studios · Research study · November 2026

Thirty years of RTS, action and MOBA enemy AI, read for one purpose: heroes against waves of mobs, with a boss at the end of each phase. The goal is enemies that are simple, fast and reliable, and still have soul.

Executive summary

  1. Soul comes from authored behavior, not clever algorithms. Enemies that felt alive came from readable behavior chosen at the right moment, throttled for fairness and paced by a director. Elden Ring's bosses run on "low-tech" state machines and priority scripts in a Lua dialect V, and F.E.A.R.'s "smart" soldiers owed much of their reputation to barks that announced what they were doing V.
  2. Use five layers, all of them cheap. A credit-and-intensity director decides when and what to spawn. An authored wave commander gives each phase its identity. Tiny FSM or utility mob brains take turns through attack tokens. A shared flow field and influence map handles movement. A boss brain runs HP-threshold phases, weighted moves, shared cooldowns, telegraphs and a little memory I.
  3. The best-scoring technique is a counter. Attack tokens, the "circle of hate", score 24/25 in the scorecard. Directors, threat tables and personality profiles follow. They give high soul with high control at near-zero cost I.
  4. Learned models work best as small, boxed selectors. Supreme Commander 2 shipped four neural nets that cost at most 0.03 ms each and decided only "fight or flee, and whom to hit" V. The pattern that works: code lists the legal options, the model only chooses, and code checks and executes the choice.
  5. The decision-model wave of September–October 2026 makes a neural boss brain possible, not a neural mob. At least six products launched: Jev, OpenAI Decisions API, Cloudflare Clef and Clef-flash, Fastino GLiDE and GLiNER2.5-Decide, and Liquid AI d1. At about 8 ms per question (d1-3B, vendor figure), a boss asking every 1–2 s fits if the call is asynchronous and runs on the host. Hundreds of mobs do not fit I. Every published benchmark is vendor-reported, and none measures game play V.
  6. Deterministic body, probabilistic mind. Authored moves with telegraphs and cooldowns, a legality mask, a model or table that picks, a seeded RNG that samples, and a weighted-random fallback. The model can only return moves you offered, so it cannot be unfair or unreadable I.
  7. Before any neural model, try a contextual bandit or Dynamic Scripting. They give most of the "it adapts to us" effect with zero dependencies I. Deep RL belongs in offline playtesting. World models such as JEPA do not apply, because your game already is its own world model I.

Scripts, rules and state machines built the genre's habits

Early RTS AI was mostly scripted build orders plus scripted or triggered attack waves, and its most common cheat was ignoring fog of war V (Game Developer: AI of C&C). The flaws players remember are rigid attack directions, idle bases waiting for a trigger, and fixed rush scripts. The strengths are what WC3 custom maps were built on: cheap, bounded, tunable behavior that players could learn and master.

1.1Timed triggers and scripted waves›Every N seconds, spawn these units here and send them there. When the boss drops below X% HP, do Y.
How it works
Event → condition → action rules on timers and regions. The canonical WC3 custom-map wave is three triggers: a periodic timer creates units at a spawn region, an immediate attack-move orders them to a waypoint, and region-enter triggers issue the next waypoint V (Hive: hero defense move trigger). Boss scripting on the Hive is the same idea: an "Every X seconds" event (10 s in the example) orders the boss to cast, random strike points sit 200–600 units from the boss, dummy units do the casting, and one-shot HP-threshold triggers set a phase boolean and then disable themselves so healing cannot retrigger them V (Hive: How to make a boss fight; Hive: boss battle advice).
Cost
Near zero. When WC3 maps lagged, the cause was how the triggers were written: per-unit timers, order spam to one player slot, new groups every tick, square roots. The community fixes were squared-distance compares, global groups, splitting units across player slots, and returning whole camps at once V (Hive: laggy creep return system).
Games that used it
  • StarCraft's campaign AI sends periodic scripted forces "regardless of player actions" and is "designed to lose while giving the player some fun" V. StarEdit shipped scripts named "Send All Units on Random Suicide Missions", the ancestor of custom-map waves V. StarCraft Wiki: AI script
  • Warcraft II campaign bases reportedly sat idle until the player crossed a trigger point U, per player testimony. GOG forum
  • Legion TD began as a WC3 World Editor map V. Wikipedia: Legion TD
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Attempt one is exciting and attempt three is a metronome. A boss that casts Meteor every 10 s teaches the team the rhythm almost immediately. Nothing in the boss reacts to you, so the fight feels like a hazard course rather than an opponent. Waves feel like a conveyor belt: dramatic when the composition is clever, mindless when it is not.
Risks
Players exploit fixed triggers by never tripping them. WC3's engine-level creep "guard/return" behavior fights wave orders and produces the infamous units-return-to-spawn bug; the fix was a computer player slot, a large acquisition range and nulled return constants V (Hive: units return after attacking). Re-issuing move orders every 10–20 s made units "run back and forth" and stall V.
1.2Declarative rule systems (Age of Empires II .per)›A list of "if these facts are true, do this" rules, plus a dashboard of numeric knobs.
How it works
Forward-chaining production rules of the form (defrule <conditions> => <actions>), for example (unit-type-count-total knight-line < 10) => (train knight-line). (disable-self) makes a rule fire once. Strategic numbers such as sn-percent-attack-soldiers tune behavior without code V (AoE Forums: three ways to attack). A general rule-based expert system is the same production-system idea with Rete-style matching K (Forgy 1982).
Cost
Cheap to evaluate. Balancing per difficulty means changing data, not logic.
Games that used it
Age of Empires II and its DE remaster. In 2026 players still describe AoE2 DE's AI as running "one generic effective build order" that is "effective but dull" V. AoE Forums: rules AoE Forums: effective but dull?
Feel in a Hero Defense
I A competent drill sergeant: correct, consistent, slightly boring. Rule systems are excellent for a wave commander's data file ("if a tower falls in lane 2, divert 30% of the next wave to lane 2") and poor at producing individual character.
Risks
Rule interactions grow opaque as the rulebase grows. A "strong but single-minded" AI reads as dull, and AoE players asked explicitly for personalities such as aggressor versus builder V.
1.3Finite state machines (FSM)›The enemy is always in exactly one mood (idle, chasing, attacking, fleeing), and specific events move it between moods.
How it works
Discrete states with explicit transitions, stacked by time scale.
  • C&C's strategic layer. Each computer "House" cycles through five states (Build Up, Broke, Threatened, Attacked, Endgame) and re-evaluates its state and enemy at most every 10 seconds, with house behavior at 15 fps.
  • Red Alert's per-unit layer added per-infantry fear states; scared units become unresponsive. The code comment reads: "When in darkness or in doubt, run in circles, scream, and shout!" V (Game Developer: AI of C&C)
  • WC3 creeps run an implicit per-unit FSM: Sleep (at night) → Idle/Guard → Attack → Chase (limited distance) → Return → Idle. Level 7+ creeps add a "flee from towers below 60% HP" transition V (Warcraft Wiki: Creeps).
Cost
The cheapest decision model there is. Most ticks cost a few distance checks.
Games that used it
C&C source analysis WC3 creeps (Warcraft Wiki) Blizzard creeping guide (Hive mirror) Elden Ring's low-tech AI
Elden Ring's enemy AI is still "basic state machines, priority-based decision trees and straightforward scripting" in Havok Script, a Lua dialect V.
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Reliable and legible. You see the gnoll go from asleep to awake to angry, and you learn to pull it. C&C's last-stand behavior gives drama for free: when the AI loses its construction yard it sells everything and sends every unit to hunt V. That is the ancestor of every boss enrage.
Risks
State explosion: every new nuance multiplies the transitions. Implement stances (aggressive, defensive, hold) as parameters on one FSM, not as new states I. Over-rigid machines become "too easy to predict, or even seemingly random" V (GameDev.net, Howland). FSMs "are easily exploitable by adaptive opponents" V (Ontañón et al.).
1.3bHierarchical FSM (HFSM)›States that contain smaller state machines. One parent per boss phase, each with its own move machine.
How it works
States contain sub-machines, for example Phase2 { Chase, Attack, Enrage }. Transitions on the parent apply to all children, which removes the state explosion of flat FSMs K (Harel 1987, statecharts). It is the natural shape for boss phases: one parent state per HP phase, each with its own small move machine.
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Clear, learnable phase identity.
Source
Supplement profile; Left 4 Dead's "concurrent HFSM behaviors" are a shipped example V (Booth).
1.4Engine-level aggro rules: acquire, assist, leash, level-gated targeting›A few one-line rules about whom to notice, whom to help and how far to chase. Players turn them into a whole skill set.
How it works
WC3 creeps: a per-unit acquisition range, "commonly 200 or 500"; camp-wide assist (attack one and the whole camp joins); limited chase, then return; sleep at night; level-gated targeting (low-level creeps focus one unit and chase a fleeing unit before giving up, level 7+ creeps prioritize injured units and heroes) V (Warcraft Wiki; Blizzard creeping guide). The global "Creeps – Guard Distance" constant works as a call-for-help radius V (Hive: guard distance).
Dota 2 lane creeps: attack the nearest enemy, and switch to an enemy hero who orders an attack on one of their own side's heroes within about 500 units. That switch lasts about 2.3 s and has about a 3 s cooldown Vsecondary guide (Hotspawn). Neutral camps respawn at each minute mark only if the spawn box is empty V (Liquipedia: Pulling).
Cost
Trivial: event-driven checks.
Games that used it
warcraft3.info: Interacting With Creeps Liquipedia: Pulling Warcraft Wiki: Leash
Feel in a Hero Defense
I This is where "soul from simple rules" first appears. In WC3, creeps "direct their anger" at summoned units, so the official advice is to send summons in first; Golems throw boulders at heroes; creeps do not walk out of Blizzard or Flame Strike V. Each quirk is one line of logic, and each becomes a technique players are proud of. Dota's 3-second aggro cooldown gives "one or two free hits" of harass, and stacking at xx:53 became a staple support skill V.
Risks
Leash and chase-give-up rules are both safety valves and exploits. Players kite mobs to the edge of the leash, and WoW raid bosses auto-reset when pulled out of their arena as a counter V. Model "camp guard (leashed)" and "wave marcher (no leash, objective-driven)" as two explicit modes so the WC3 return-to-spawn bug cannot happen I.
1.5Personality profiles on a shared rule engine (AMAI, SC2 named builds)›One AI engine, many characters: change the parameters, not the code.
How it works
AMAI, the community WC3 melee AI, is written in JASS. It assesses enemy units, game duration and its profile's favoured units. Profiles are personalities: some cautious, some rush, some surrender when losing. It focus-fires vulnerable units, retreats via Town Portal, and taunts in 10 languages V (AMAI GitHub). StarCraft II offers per-slot named builds ("Full Rush", "Timing Attack", "Economic Focus", "Baneling Bust Timing"), and its AI "announces its current strategy to allies" V (Liquipedia: SC2 AI).
Cost
Same as the underlying engine. AMAI uses more resources than the default AI and risks lag at 24 players V.
Games that used it
AMAI on GitHub Liquipedia: SC2 AI
Feel in a Hero Defense
I A rival with a name. "Grukk the Patient" holds back elites until your mana is spent, while "Vashka the Hungry" dives the healer. Same code, different weights, different stories.
Risks
Personalities only read if they are visible. Announce them with a name card, an opening bark, or a characteristic first move.

Trees, planners, scores and directors made AI readable at scale

The second generation came from action games, not RTS. It solved three problems: organizing hundreds of behaviors without spaghetti (behavior trees), letting enemies improvise (GOAP, HTN), and choosing gracefully between options (utility). Then it added a fourth idea that matters most for wave games: an AI director that manages the player's experience instead of any single enemy.

2.1Behavior trees (BT)›A flowchart of priorities. Try the most important thing first, and fall back if it fails.
How it works
A tree of selectors, which "find and execute the first child that does not fail", and sequences, which run children in order until one fails, is ticked from the root. Each node returns running, success or failure V (Wikipedia: Behavior tree). Event-driven BTs add nodes that react to events and abort running branches, which fixes the cost of re-ticking the whole tree V (Game AI Pro BT Starter Kit). BTs are built into Unity and Unreal V.
Cost
Cheap if most mobs run small trees and ticks are spread across frames I.
Games that used it
  • BTs entered mainstream game AI through Damián Isla's GDC 2005 Halo 2 talk and are "extensively used in … Halo, Bioshock, and Spore" V. Halo 2 internals are U: the primary article could not be fetched. Wikipedia: Behavior tree Bungie on Halo AI
  • Alien: Isolation's xenomorph uses a BT of over 100 nodes. Some branches start locked and unlock as the campaign progresses, so the alien seems to learn, and unlocks are never tied to player deaths V. The Perfect Organism
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Readable and reliable. Players learn "when it is low it retreats to the shaman", which suits telegraphed boss phases. Alien: Isolation's branch unlocking maps neatly onto boss phases: phase 2 unlocks the Charge branch, phase 3 unlocks Summon. The soul comes from Isla's principles rather than from the tree itself: fallible perception ("Each AI has an internal model of each target, and that model can be wrong… This allows the AI to be surprised by you, and this is very fun") and good mistakes versus bad mistakes (a Hunter whiffing a melee and opening itself to a flank is good; NPCs walking into walls is bad) V.
Risks
Re-evaluating from the root wastes CPU. The tree loses context across branch switches: in Halo 2, "if an AI tips over his vehicle, he walks off and forgets completely he was ever in one" V. Strict priority order is all-or-nothing, which is why people add utility selectors (2.4).
2.2Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP)›Give the enemy goals and a menu of actions with preconditions and effects, and it works out a plan by itself.
How it works
Jeff Orkin's F.E.A.R. design: "the Finite State Machine has only three states, and A* is used to plan actions as well as paths" V (GDC Vault: Three States and a Plan). The planner searches backward from the goal through action preconditions and effects, and replans when the world changes. If the player slams a door, "the NPC replans and decides to kick the door in" V (GameSpot: F.E.A.R. Designer Diary #2). A squad layer "supersedes and directs the individual's movement" V. GOAP is a game-tuned STRIPS plus A* K (Fikes & Nilsson 1971); pure STRIPS is rarely used directly in games.
Cost
An A* search over actions per agent is expensive next to a BT tick; it suits a few elites or a squad leader, not 300 mobs I. Total War's Empire, Napoleon and Shogun 2 campaign AIs used GOAP; Shogun 2's reactive planner struggled when DLC added naval logic V (Game Developer: AI of Total War Part 3).
Games that used it
F.E.A.R. Later adopters such as Tomb Raider (2013) and Shadow of Mordor are commonly cited but U. GDC Vault: Three States and a Plan F.E.A.R. designer diary
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Surprising, as if the enemy "figured something out". An orc warband that finds the wall blocked and goes round through the forest gate feels clever. The F.E.A.R. team's real lesson is cheaper than GOAP: barks that verbalize intent. "He's trying to flank!" and "We've got two men down!" were "extremely accurate" to the AI's actual state V. Whether players perceive the intelligence matters more than the intelligence itself V (Digitec).
Risks
Unpredictable plans make authored boss choreography harder, and "unpredictable results make some developers uneasy" V. The planner is harder to debug than a tree.
2.3Hierarchical Task Network planning (HTN)›Planning with a playbook. The designer writes the recipes ("to assault: suppress, flank, charge"), and the AI picks which recipe fits.
How it works
Compound tasks decompose into primitive actions through designer-authored methods, with backtracking over preconditions. Guerrilla describes its Decima HTN planner as similar to Prolog, with in-game decomposition debugging V (Guerrilla: HTN Planning in Decima). Killzone 2's multiplayer bots used one AI commander per faction → group leaders → individual bots V (Guerrilla: layered MP bots).
Cost
Faster and more bounded than GOAP's open search. Real-time constraints limit planning in RTS V.
Games that used it
Killzone 2 through Horizon (Decima); Transformers: Fall of Cybertron won an AiGameDev 2012 award for its HTN V. Guerrilla: HTN in Decima DigiPen News: Transformers AI Fluid HTN for Unity (open source)
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Like facing a faction with doctrine. The undead always "pin with ghouls, then flank with gargoyles". The orcs "probe every lane, then all-in the weakest". Players learn the doctrine but cannot predict the exact execution. HTN fits best at the wave-commander level, while individual mobs stay on FSMs.
Risks
Authoring overhead, and planner debugging needs tooling. Overkill for fodder.
2.4Utility AI (scoring and response curves)›Score every option from 0 to 1 using a few considerations, then pick the best, or pick randomly among the best few.
How it works
  • Normalize each consideration to 0–1 ("2.0 means nothing by itself"), shape it with response curves (linear, logistic, S-curve), weight it, and combine.
  • Commitment. Dill: "Add utility to what you're doing so you stick to your decision." Vetoes: a consideration can zero out an option, for example no ammo means no shooting V (Game Developer: GDC 2010 AI Summit).
  • Personality through weights. The same consideration categories "get weighted differently" per NPC V.
  • Controlled randomness. The Sims 3 used "a modified version of the Boltzmann distribution", a temperature-controlled weighted random pick V (Wikipedia: Utility system).
  • Data-driven at scale. Dave Mark and Mike Lewis's Infinite Axis Utility System (GDC Vault: Building a Better Centaur). Its use in Guild Wars 2 is U.
Cost
N actions × M considerations per decision; fine at 2–10 Hz for hundreds of mobs if the curves are lookup tables I.
Games that used it
The Sims series, "one of the first visible uses of utility AI" V. Alien: Isolation's utility "job system", also used for BioShock Infinite's Elizabeth V. Wikipedia: Utility system Alien: Isolation AI
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Organic. The wounded archer kites, the brute charges the lowest-HP hero, the shaman heals only when allies are really hurt. Players rarely see a hard rule, so the mobs feel alive but are less learnable than with an FSM. Utility is the natural engine for target selection and boss ability choice.
Risks
Tuning curves × weights is a "dark art", and odd choices are hard to trace without a per-agent score readout. Dithering, where the agent flips between options each tick, is the classic failure; commitment bonuses and vetoes fix it V. Rule of thumb from the supplement I: give the current action +10–25% utility and require a minimum commitment time of 0.5–1.5 s before re-deciding, except on interrupts (stun, big damage).
2.5AI directors: intensity pacing and credit budgets›A dungeon master that watches how stressed the players are and decides when the next wave hits, how big it is, and when everyone gets a breather.
How it works
Left 4 Dead (Valve, 2008). The director tracks per-survivor emotional intensity. It rises with damage taken, incapacitation and nearby kills, and decays when the survivors are not engaged. It cycles Build Up (full threat population until intensity peaks) → Sustain Peak (3–5 s) → Peak Fade (minimal population while intensity decays) → Relax (minimal population for 30–45 s or a travel distance). Mobs are 20–30 common infected every 90–180 s on Normal, and about 75% of them spawn behind the team. Bosses are exempt from adaptive pacing. "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty" V (Booth, AI Systems of L4D; Valve PDF).
Risk of Rain 2 credit directors. Directors earn credits and spend them on weighted spawn cards. Example costs: Beetle 8, Lemurian 11, Greater Wisp 200, Grandparent 1150. Each card has a weight, a cost and an earliest-stage condition. The normal directors switch off 3 s after the Teleporter event starts, and a separate Boss Director switches on 3 s after V (RoR2 wiki: Directors).
Alien: Isolation's "two brains". A director that always knows where the player is drives a menace gauge and only "points the alien in the direction of the player". The alien must still find the player with its own senses. At peak menace the director pulls the alien back into the vents V.
Cost
One slow-ticking meta-agent. L4D's Active Area Set let "a small set of reused entities" stand in for hundreds of infected V.
Games that used it
RimWorld's storytellers are commonly cited but U here. L4D: Booth slides RoR2 wiki: Directors Alien: Isolation AI
Feel in a Hero Defense
I The director is what makes the game feel as if it "read my mind". After the team barely survives the wave-7 elite push, the director holds back and the shop window feels earned. When the heroes are bored and full HP, a flanking pack arrives from the side gate. Gabe Newell called this "procedural narrative", a "story-telling device" rather than a difficulty mechanism V (Wikipedia: Left 4 Dead).
Risks
Visible rubber-banding feels like cheating. Booth's "pacing, not difficulty" rule and Alien's "director hints, the agent must still find you" rule both contain it.

31987 (boids) · 2006–2015

Spatial and group tactics turn a crowd into a fighting force

Gen 2 decided what to do. Gen 3 decides where to stand and who gets to swing. With dozens of mobs converging on four heroes, this layer drives most of the felt difference between a "conga line of idiots" and "they surrounded us".

3.1Steering, flocking, flow fields and local avoidance›Each unit nudges itself away from neighbours and toward the goal. A shared "arrow map" tells everyone which way is downhill to the target.
How it works
  • Boids (Reynolds 1987) use three prioritized rules: separation, alignment and cohesion. Perception is local; an early global-centre version collapsed the flock to a point V (ACM SIGGRAPH HyperGraph).
  • Flow fields. One integration field per destination is shared by every unit going there. In Supreme Commander 2, per third-party reverse engineering, moving "a group of 100 units has the same flowfield-generation cost as moving 1 unit", with 5 bits per tile Vunofficial analysis (Wildfire Games forum).
  • ORCA/RVO2. Each agent takes half the responsibility for avoiding each pairwise collision; 25,000 agents in "a few milliseconds" per update V (UNC GAMMA ORCA).
  • Continuum Crowds unifies navigation and avoidance in one potential field, but a thesis benchmark ran 4,500 units at only about 6 fps on a Core 2 Duo V (Deeb, DigiPen thesis).
Cost
Flow fields plus separation over a spatial grid cost little for 50–300 mobs. Continuum Crowds is overkill I.
Games that used it
  • Left 4 Dead uses reactive path following (steer to a look-ahead point) because optimized paths looked "robotic" V. Booth PDF
  • StarCraft II units "push through their buddies rather than repath", and zerglings "compress towards a stream" Vcommunity observation. gamedev.net thread
  • Wolcen uses context steering and chooses routes for aesthetics ("Bloodtrail") V. GDC Vault: AI in Wolcen
  • They Are Billions claims to move "up to 20,000 units simultaneously" Vdeveloper claim. Numantian Games
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Strong separation and slightly varied speeds make a wave look like an organic crowd that spills through chokes and wraps around the tank. With no separation you get a single-file conga line. Perfect ORCA looks eerily polite; some shoving, SC2-style, reads as "hungry". A flow field computed from each hero gives every mob a gradient from any direction, so flanking through alternate routes emerges by itself.
Risks
Jams at chokes, oscillation, and body-blocking 30 mobs on one hero, the WC3 classic. Attack slots (3.4) solve the last one.
3.2Influence maps›A shared heat map of "where the heroes can hurt me" and "where my friends are". Each mob asks it a simple question, such as "where is the safest spot near me?"
How it works
Dave Mark's modular design in Game AI Pro 2 ch. 30 V (Mark, GAIP2 ch. 30):
  • Templates. Per-faction proximity and threat maps are built by "stamping" precomputed templates: a 21×21 proximity template for a 10 m/s unit with a 1 s update, and a 61×61 threat template for a 30 m spell range.
  • Update rate. Every 1 s; nothing faster than 0.5 s and nothing slower than 2 s.
  • Layer algebra. Add, multiply, invert (1 − v) and normalize. Retreat = inverse(enemy threat) × interest → max cell. AoE targeting = enemy location × interest → max cell. Battlefront = enemy threat × ally threat. Spacing = subtract 0.5 × ally proximity.
Cost
Cheap. Agents read only small windows centred on themselves, and maps update at 1 Hz VI.
Games that used it
The research found no confirmed shipped RTS using it for threat or front lines U. GAIP2 ch. 30 Demo videos thread
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Mobs seem to "understand the battlefield": archers hang at the edge of the Blademaster's reach, wounded goblins slink away to where no hero stands, the warlock drops its Rain of Fire exactly where your heroes have clumped. The 0.5–2 s staleness reads as natural hesitation, not aimbot precision.
Risks
Tuning the layer weights. Overly perfect evasion feels cheap, so add reaction delay and per-mob dodge odds.
3.3Potential fields›Every object pulls or pushes. Each unit steps to whichever neighbouring tile "feels" best.
How it works
Hagelbäck and Johansson's ORTS bot V (AAMAS 2008). Enemies emit a ring of maximum attraction at the unit's own Maximum Shooting Distance (MSD), so ranged units hold max range and melee units close in. Terrain repels at −80/d² and allies repel at −20 within the unit radius. Each unit picks the best of its 8 neighbours, at most 9 evaluations per unit, with a random walk to escape local optima. An anti-overkill coordinator sorts targets by HP and assigns "just enough" attackers.
Cost
Mostly array sums over precomputed fields.
Games that used it
The ORTS 2007 bot, the StarCraft bot BTHAI, and Overmind's mutalisk harassment in the 2010 AIIDE StarCraft winner V. BTHAI entry Competition history
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Mobs form a living ring around each hero at their own preferred range; archers "respect your reach". Add the hero's threat as a repulsor during the mob's weapon cooldown and you get kiting almost for free. The ORTS winner (NUS, 98–99% win rates) did exactly that V.
Risks
The first PF bot won only about 28–32% of its ORTS matches; the authors blamed local optima, lack of squad cohesion, and summed enemy fields pulling units into enemy clusters V. Jitter and random-walk wandering look aimless.
3.4Attack tokens and the "circle of hate"›Only a few enemies may attack you at once. The rest circle, posture and wait their turn.
How it works
  • DOOM 2016. Each attack type (melee, ranged, charged) has a limited token pool. A demon must request a token before attacking and return it afterwards. Token counts scale with difficulty, and demons can steal tokens from each other when better placed, so the demons in front of the player don't "stand around looking stupid" V (Game Developer: AI of DOOM 2016).
  • Practitioner defaults for a UE5 implementation Vpractitioner blog (StraySpark): pools per target and category (melee 1, ranged 2, heavy 1); claim the token on entering the attack state, not mid-telegraph; one 0–1 aggression budget scales every pool from 0.5× to 1.5×; an audit about every 2 s reclaims tokens leaked by dead or stuck agents.
  • "A* circle of hate" (Chris Jurney, GDC 2010). "Is player within enemy's A* circle of hate? Attack!" "Overlapping circles aren't allowed, preventing player swampage." "Designers control the size of hate radius, which also helps sync animation." V (Game Developer: GDC 2010 Day 1.1). Whether it comes from Dawn of War 2 or Brütal Legend is ambiguous (GDC Vault session).
Cost
A counter per hero: the cheapest high-impact technique in this study I.
Games that used it
Batman: Arkham also has fairness locks: other enemies don't attack during takedown animations, and the last enemy falls in one hit at a 2× combo V. DOOM 2016 AI Batman: Arkham analysis
Feel in a Hero Defense
I The fight looks choreographed and readable instead of a blob. Three ghouls claw the Paladin while five more prowl the ring, snarling, and one darts in the instant a slot opens. Pressure stays constant but survivable, and every swing has a visible owner. Waiting mobs can get personality "without more animation", as Jurney put it: weak units "seek cover and wait for backup" V. Token count is also a much cleaner difficulty knob than raw mob HP.
Risks
Leaked tokens make enemies stop attacking, so audit them. If the waiting mobs idle, the effect looks stupid, so they must move.
3.5Threat tables, focus fire and target priority›The enemy remembers who hurt it most, and you can manipulate that.
How it works
WoW Classic's model V (Warcraft Tavern threat guide): one damage equals one threat; healing generates half threat, split across engaged mobs; threat never decays; aggro switches only when someone reaches 110% of the current target's threat in melee range, or 130% at range; taunt sets the taunter's threat equal to the top of the table. Compare RTS focus fire, which sorts targets by HP and assigns just enough attackers to kill this frame V.
Cost
A small array per boss or elite.
Games that used it
Warcraft Tavern: threat Liquipedia: Roshan
Feel in a Hero Defense
I A threat table makes the boss hate someone. Tanks get a job, healers fear over-healing, and DPS learn restraint. The 110/130% hysteresis keeps target swaps readable and fair, with no ping-pong. Mix rules by enemy type: fodder hits the nearest target, assassins pick the squishiest in reach, bosses use the threat table.
Risks
Threat effectively caps DPS ("raids throttle damage") V. A pure threat table makes bosses passive puppets of the tank, so break it deliberately and with a telegraph ("fixates the healer for 8 s").
42003–2020 · academic

Academic search and adaptation beat scripts only where the model is accurate

From Buro's 2003 call to treat RTS as a grand-challenge AI problem, through ORTS, Wargus and StarCraft via BWAPI, academia spent two decades on RTS AI V (Game Developer: brief history of RTS AI research). Its sobering conclusion: hand-made scripts plus narrow, budgeted search or learning beat end-to-end approaches, and humans find and exploit scripted behavior within a few games.

4.1Combat search: ABCD, UCTCD, Portfolio Greedy Search, MCTS›Before committing, the AI quickly simulates "what if I do this?" a few hundred times and picks the best result.
How it works
  • ABCD (alpha-beta with durations) searches small battles (2–8 units per side) with a 5 ms budget inside a 50 ms frame. Against scripts it scored 0.92 on average using playout evaluation V (Churchill et al., AIIDE 2012).
  • Portfolio Greedy Search (PGS) searches over which authored script each unit runs (for example NOK-AV, which avoids overkill, or Kiter), not over raw actions. Linear in units, 40 ms budget. Against Alpha-Beta it scored 0.319 at 8v8 but 0.990 at 50v50 V (Churchill & Buro, combat13).
  • MCTS in Total War: Rome II allocated campaign tasks but "was only capable of looking one turn ahead before starting random playouts", and AI turns took "several minutes" at launch V (Game Developer: AI of Total War Part 3).
Cost
5–40 ms per search, which at 60 fps must be time-sliced or run at 2–5 Hz I. Forward-model fidelity is the binding constraint. In real StarCraft, collisions made the simulator inaccurate in large ground battles, and the simple AttackClosest script was hard to beat. PGS won where the simulator matched reality, as with flying units V (AIIDE PGS analysis).
Games that used it
UAlbertaBot added the SparCraft combat simulator to decide attack or retreat V. Competition history Total War AI, part 3
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Sharp but sometimes cowardly. An elite squad that retreats whenever it predicts a loss feels timid or broken. In a 2015 human-vs-bot exhibition, tscmoo's simulator predicted a loss, its Hydralisks scattered, and Psionic Storm destroyed the split army V (AIIDE 2015 report). In 2017, pro player Song Byung-gu said bots "avoided bold decisions" V (MIT Technology Review). Used as a PGS-style choice among your authored scripts, search gives an elite squad or a boss real tactical bite while staying readable.
Risks
Simulator bugs get exploited: a human beat UAlbertaBot partly because its simulator ignored the Dragoon range upgrade V. It costs CPU, and it biases enemies toward caution unless the evaluation rewards commitment.
4.2Dynamic Scripting with top culling›Each fight, the enemy draws a hand of tactics from a weighted deck of authored tactics. Tactics that worked gain weight; tactics that failed lose it.
How it works
Spronck et al. V (Machine Learning 2006; PDF). Each opponent type has a rulebase. Each encounter's script is drawn by weight-proportional selection: 5 rules out of 20 for fighters, 10 out of 50 for wizards. After the fight, rules that fired are rewarded or penalized, and the total weight stays constant. Parameters: initial weight 100, max weight 2000, max reward 100, max penalty 30–50. Top culling (rules above Wmax cannot be selected) kept the AI at an even game against all 8 test tactics: in Neverwinter Nights it averaged 49.8 wins out of 100 (SD 3.4), against 79.4 (SD 12.7) for plain optimization V (Spronck difficulty scaling).
Cost
One weighted draw and one weight update per encounter: effectively free.
Games that used it
Neverwinter Nights experiments. A related idea, UCB-1 bandit selection among strategies across games, was used by UAlbertaBot V. Spronck PDF
Feel in a Hero Defense
I "They're adapting to us." If your party wins wave after wave by stacking behind the Mountain King, the warlocks' "AoE on the cluster" rule gains weight and "spread and flank" fires more often. Every tactic stays hand-authored, readable and bug-checked. With top culling it never becomes oppressive: the enemy gets smarter or dumber, not statistically stronger.
Risks
Slow within a session: the average turning point was 34 encounters (median 27, range 10–101) V. Seed sensible weights, treat each wave or phase as an encounter, or persist weights across sessions I.
4.3Dynamic difficulty adjustment (DDA)›Quietly make the next fight a bit easier or harder depending on how the players are doing.
How it works
Hunicke and Chapman's Hamlet (Half-Life) separates reactive adjustments (changing enemies already on screen) from proactive ones (changing enemies not yet seen), and warns that reactive changes make the game "appear schizophrenic" V (Hamlet.pdf). A wave-defence study ("Colwell's Castle Defence") added +1 to +5 enemies to the next level based on player and gate health; speed and damage changes were rejected because testers noticed them. Enjoyment rose from 5.8 to 8.0 for weak players and from 5.9 to 8.3 for strong ones, with only 30 participants V (arXiv 1806.04471). Resident Evil 4's hidden adaptive difficulty is widely reported, but Capcom "never confirmed the feature's existence" Vthat it is unconfirmed (Engadget).
Cost
Trivial.
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Done right, it feels like nothing at all: the game just flows. Done wrong, players notice the gnoll that suddenly hits harder mid-fight and feel cheated.
Risks
Spiralling loops of adjustment: proactive changes "may actually require subsequent reactive adjustments" V.
51996–2016 · plus lightweight ML

Small neural nets and case libraries shipped learned behavior in a box

Contrary to the hype cycle, learned AI has shipped in games for 30 years, almost always as small models in narrow roles, wrapped in authored control layers.

5.1Small MLPs for narrow judgements (Supreme Commander 2, Planetary Annihilation)›A tiny neural net answers one question: "is this fight worth taking, and what should I hit?" Everything else is normal game code.
How it works
Supreme Commander 2 (2010) shipped four MLPs (land, naval, bomber and fighter platoons) V (Robbins, Game AI Pro ch. 30). Each has 34 inputs (friendly/enemy ratios of count, health, DPS, speed and shields within a radius), 98 hidden nodes and 15 outputs, each estimating the utility of a targeting action. The platoon takes the highest output and retreats if no output exceeds 0.5. Training: backprop on AI-vs-AI fights run "as fast as possible", with random-action exploration and a one-step fitness, about 1 hour per network. Runtime: "never above 0.03 ms per network in an eight-player AI match". Inflating the input ratios makes the net overestimate its odds. Planetary Annihilation's AI, by the same developer ("Sorian"), also uses NNs for attack and retreat; biasing inputs makes difficulty look like "overconfidence or timidness" rather than stupidity V (PA patch notes; PA forums).
Cost
Effectively free: about 4.8k weights each, by arithmetic (34×98 + 98×15).
Games that used it
  • SupCom 2 and Planetary Annihilation. Game AI Pro ch. 30 PA patch notes
  • Creatures (1996): 952 neurons and about 5,000 connections per Norn brain V. Zucconi: AI of Creatures
  • Forza Drivatar: small Bayesian nets, "essentially a list of weights", wrapped in a control layer that reduces aggression and prevents rams V. How Drivatar works
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Self-preserving and opportunistic. Mob groups commit when the local odds favour them and break and run when outmatched. The 0.5 retreat threshold creates readable "they broke!" moments. Input inflation doubles as a boss rage knob: an enraged boss overestimates its odds and stops retreating.
Risks
The ACU bug. Killing an enemy commander triggers a nuclear blast, so the nets learned to flee commanders; a hand-written override (double the output when an ACU is the target) fixed it, after which the AI "committed large groups and won games" V. It is a black box: breakpoints don't help, designers can't easily tune it, and any change to inputs or outputs means retraining. The author says it suits "well-defined responses and little need for designer control" V. Lockstep risk: a 0.5 threshold is exactly where a one-ulp float difference between machines flips a branch, so use fixed-point or int8 inference, or run on the host only I. A hand-tuned utility curve over the same ratios probably gets about 80% of the effect with full designer control I.
5.2Case-based reasoning: Killer Instinct "Shadows"›The AI keeps a scrapbook of what a real player did in similar situations, and does that.
How it works
Killer Instinct's Shadow AI (Iron Galaxy, 2016) V (Game Developer: The Killer Groove): records every movement, block and combo, down to the animation frame, with match state. Each match is parsed into about 400–700 "patterns", stored relative to the opponent so they transfer. Retrieval is nearest-neighbour over 40+ metrics. Up to 40 matches (about 28,000 patterns) per matchup; 3 dojo matches give base competence. It ranks options by value but occasionally picks a lower-ranked action to trick the opponent, and revises priorities mid-match if a move keeps getting punished.
Cost
k-NN over a few thousand cases is cheap. With integer metrics and a seeded RNG it is deterministic by construction I.
Games that used it
Academic precedents include case-based planning in Wargus (the WC2 clone) and Darmok V. The Killer Groove
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Uncanny: "it fights like him". A boss whose patterns were recorded from your best designer's playtests, or from the party's own past attempts, has a recognizable style. It bluffs, and when you keep punishing its leap it stops leaping. The mid-fight revision step is a natural multi-phase mechanic: phase 2 down-weights whatever killed it in phase 1.
Risks
It needs good recorded data and competitive designer expertise. A behavior must be seen several times before it is learned, and the 40-match cap was insufficient for some behaviors V.

62019–2023

Deep RL reached pro level but never shipped as an RTS enemy

6Deep reinforcement learning (self-play)›Let the AI play itself millions of times until it is superhuman.
How it works
Self-play RL at datacenter scale, with action masks, entity attention and human-like caps.
  • AlphaStar (DeepMind, 2019) reached Grandmaster with all three StarCraft II races, above 99.8% of active players, capped at 22 agent actions per 5 s and playing through a camera interface. League training added "exploiter" agents to find weaknesses V.
  • OpenAI Five used a single 4,096-unit LSTM per hero, trained with 128,000 CPU cores and 256 GPUs, and beat world champions OG in April 2019 V.
  • Tencent's Honor of Kings agent used about 600,000 CPU cores and 1,064 GPUs, action masks for illegal actions and attention-based target selection. It acts every 133 ms and won 2,096 of 2,100 public games V.
  • GT Sophy 2.0 is the main shipped deep-RL opponent: it reached GT7 globally in November 2023, tuned for "sportsmanship" and all skill levels V.
Cost
Inference is cheap: one forward pass every 133–200 ms per agent. Training is out of indie reach I. Indie routes exist: Unity ML-Agents exports ONNX, and Unity recommends CPU (Burst) inference for most ML-Agents models as typically faster than GPU V (Unity docs); Godot RL Agents (MIT licence) has experimental ONNX export V (GitHub); DreamerV3 collected Minecraft diamonds from scratch in 17 days on one V100 V (The Decoder).
Games that used it
AlphaStar OpenAI Five Honor of Kings (arXiv) GT Sophy
Feel in a Hero Defense
I Pros described AlphaStar as having "pro-level reflexes with strategies unlike humans", and MaNa "could still spot weaknesses" V. A boss trained this way would be fascinating once and alienating afterwards. It optimizes winning, not drama, and superhuman micro reads as cheating. Critics called OpenAI Five's wins "basically cheating" because of API access and restricted hero pools V.
Risks
Training cost, retraining whenever you rebalance, loss of designer control, and non-determinism. Every shipped case (Sophy, Drivatar) needed sportsmanship or aggression tuning layered on top V. The best indie use is offline: RL or self-play bots as playtesters that find degenerate hero builds, in the spirit of AlphaStar's exploiter agents I.
72025–2026

Decision models and world models are commanders, not foot soldiers

The newest generation splits in two. Language and decision models can serve as a slow "System 2" commander that chooses among legal options. Generative world models are research previews for ideation and testing, not enemy controllers. Every credible deployment found uses the same split: deterministic code at tick rate, a model at event rate.

7.1On-device small language models as "System 2" (PUBG Ally)›A small chatbot brain decides intent ("cover me, push left"). A normal behavior tree does the actual aiming and moving.
How it works
KRAFTON's PUBG Ally runs Mistral-NeMo-Minitron-2B, quantized and fully on-device on GPUs with as little as 8 GB VRAM; cloud was rejected as too slow. The SLM is an event-driven "System 2" for intent, coordination and speech. A behavior tree "System 1" handles movement, aim and combat at tick rate. Outputs are non-deterministic, so tuning relied on A/B tests and automated checks V (NVIDIA Developer Blog).
Cost
GPU memory and hundreds of milliseconds per decision; viable for one or a few commander entities, not hundreds of units I.
Games that used it
PUBG Ally public beta, June 2026. NVIDIA: PUBG Ally KRAFTON at CES 2025
Feel in a Hero Defense
I A boss that talks back in context ("Your paladin hides behind the tower again, little lord?") and coordinates its lieutenants feels uniquely alive. Its choices feel arbitrary unless they map onto telegraphed authored moves.
Risks
Non-determinism, VRAM competition with rendering, content moderation for generated speech, and a tuning burden (more than 1,000 player testers for PUBG Ally) V.

Between 15 September and early October 2026, the "decision model" category (also called "System One" models) went from one product to at least six V. All share one interface idea: you send a state plus typed questions (a yes/no predicate, a choice among options, an ordinal score), and the model returns probabilities for every allowed option in one forward pass, generating no text. For a game, that interface is exactly "choose one of my legal authored moves", the safest way to put a neural model in charge of a boss.

What this means for a Hero Defense I

  1. Per-mob, per-frame control is still out. Even 8 ms per question × hundreds of mobs blows a 16 ms frame. Mobs stay on FSM, utility and tokens.
  2. A local boss "System One brain" is now feasible. One boss asking 1–3 typed questions every 1–2 s at ~8–40 ms (d1-3B on a gaming GPU; Clef-flash self-hosted) fits if it runs asynchronously (ask now, apply the answer at the next decision tick) and only on the host. It needs a decent GPU and ~2–6 GB VRAM and adds a big dependency: fine for a PC prototype, heavy for a shipped indie game.
  3. Hosted APIs (Jev, OpenAI Decisions, Clef on Workers AI) suit a director or wave commander that decides every 5–30 s ("which counter-wave composition for this party?"), where 100–500 ms latency is invisible. They need a connection, cost money per call, and are non-deterministic: host-authoritative only, never inside the lockstep sim.
  4. Encoders like GLiNER2.5-Decide (340M, Apache-2.0) are the most shippable open option, but expect ~100+ ms on CPU; distilling them is the path to something in-engine.
  5. The design pattern is the same whatever the vendor. Because the model can only return options you offered, it cannot do anything unfair or unreadable. The soul comes from which readable move it picks and when.
  6. Honest recommendation. For most of the game, a contextual bandit or Dynamic Scripting gives about 80% of the "it adapts to us" effect with zero dependencies. Use a decision model for an experimental boss and A/B it against the bandit boss in playtests.

How it works
Decision Transformer K (Chen et al. 2021) treats RL as sequence modelling: condition on desired return ("return-to-go") and predict the next action from recent (return, state, action) tokens. The target return is a difficulty knob: ask for a lower return and get a weaker but coherent boss. It needs offline trajectory data; tiny DTs run in well under a millisecond I. The main report could not fetch the paper and found no shipped game using one U. Boss design: phases, telegraphs and a little memory

The boss is where a Hero Defense spends its drama budget. The shipped evidence converges on one structure: deterministic phase skeleton → weighted move choice inside each phase → telegraphed execution → punish windows → optional memory.

Shipped bosses run on surprisingly low-tech brains

FromSoftware writes Elden Ring's enemy AI "mostly" in Havok Script (Lua), using basic state machines, priority-based decision trees and straightforward scripting; a writeup of the decompiled code calls this "perhaps unexpectedly low-tech" V (feedbagel). A summary stresses preset thresholds, distance checks and no learning Vsecondary (80AJ summary). The commonly repeated claim that FromSoft bosses pick moves from distance-banded weighted random tables is U: no fetched source showed the odds tables or phase thresholds. Hive Workshop's WC3 boss practice is the same idea in miniature: a periodic cast timer, random strike points, dummy casters and one-shot HP triggers that set a phase boolean V.

Two ways to schedule moves: timeline versus "pick after each cast"

Classic WoW bosses were "rarely set up to have exact timers for spells". They chose their next action after each spell, partly at random, and some abilities shared cooldowns so one big ability briefly blocked the others. Blizzard admitted this made the same encounter harder or easier from week to week V (Icy Veins). Modern WoW deliberately changed encounter design "to make randomness less of a factor" V; fights are deterministic enough that addons and Blizzard's own built-in timeline display them as countdowns Vheadline only (Icy Veins headline).

Soul techniques for bosses

From the supplement; all are the author's design suggestions I, grounded in the cited games.

Personality vectors

Give every mob type and boss a vector {aggression, courage, patience, cunning, loyalty, greed} in 0–1, multiplied into utility scores, token greed and retreat thresholds. Randomise ±10% per individual for crowd variety.

Mood state machines

Calm → Agitated → Enraged → Exhausted, modulating move weights rather than replacing behavior. Each transition needs a visible tell, like Monster Hunter's glow or steam.

Episodic memory

A ring buffer of events: who hit me hardest, who stunned me, which spell hurt most, who fled. Use it for targeting ("I go after the mage who burned me") and barks. Mind the Nemesis patent.

Habit formation

When a move succeeds, raise its weight a little and persist it across waves, so each boss develops favourite moves. The maths of Dynamic Scripting and bandits, framed to the player as character.

Attack grammars

Define the moveset as a small grammar or Markov chain of combos (Opener → Pressure → Punish window). Players learn the grammar; the boss varies the sentence.

Telegraph–attack–recovery

Every move is wind-up (readable) → active frames → recovery (punish window). Tune difficulty by wind-up and recovery length, not by damage alone.

Controlled imperfection

Isla's good mistakes, Uncharted's near-misses, DOOM's accuracy drop against a fast player. Add 150–350 ms reaction jitter so mobs don't all react on the same frame.

Boss design checklist I

  1. Phase skeleton. HP thresholds (one-shot, never revert) plus a hard enrage timer.
  2. Per-phase move pool. 4–8 authored moves with weights, distance bands, cooldowns, and a shared "big move" cooldown (Classic WoW).
  3. Anti-repeat. Halve a move's weight for each recent use.
  4. Conditional moves. A cluster punish (Roshan Slam), a healer fixate, a blink onto low-HP heroes. The default WC3 AI "won't reliably blink", so script it V.
  5. Telegraph and punish. Every big move has a wind-up and a recovery window (Halo's Hunter).
  6. Rage and exhaustion. A Monster Hunter-style timed state with a visible tell, an extended move pool, a post-rage exhaustion window, and a hero counter.
  7. Threat table with telegraphed breaks. 110/130% hysteresis, and fixates announced by an emote.
  8. Adds. Spawned through the director's boss budget (RoR2's boss director handoff).
  9. Memory. A grudge flag at minimum; Dynamic Scripting or case-based reasoning for ambitious bosses.
  10. Voice. One bark per move and per phase transition.

Mob and wave design: directors spend, tokens throttle, morale ends the fight

Wave composition: authored lists plus a credit director

The proven pattern for waves that feel fair but alive is a credit-budget director V (RoR2 wiki): every enemy type has a cost and a weight; a director earns credits over time and spends them on spawn cards gated by stage; a separate boss director takes over at the phase end. Left 4 Dead adds the intensity cycle and spawns about 75% of mobs behind the team V (see the director diagram in Gen 2).

1 · Utility target selection for a mob (L3)

Score each candidate hero from 0 to 1 by multiplying shaped considerations, with vetoes and a commitment bonus (Dill/Mark, GDC 2010). Personality lives in the weights.

# Personality = weights only (e.g. goblin: finisher=2.0, healer_hate=0.5; ogre: finisher=0.3)
def choose_target(mob, heroes):
    best, best_score = None, 0.0
    for h in spatial_grid.query(mob.pos, mob.acquire_range):     # squared-distance prefilter
        if h.untargetable or h.invisible_to(mob):                 # VETO -> score 0
            continue
        d   = dist(mob.pos, h.pos) / mob.acquire_range            # 0..1
        s   = 1.0 - d                                             # closer is better (linear curve)
        s  *= logistic(1.0 - h.hp_pct, mid=0.5, k=8) ** mob.w_finisher   # smell blood
        s  *= 1.0 + mob.w_healer_hate * h.is_healer
        s  *= 1.0 / (1 + tokens.holders_on(h) * mob.w_avoid_crowd)      # spread out
        if h is mob.target:
            s *= 1.25                                             # COMMITMENT: no dithering
        if s > best_score:
            best, best_score = h, s
    return best

2 · Attack tokens with stealing and audit (L3)

Following DOOM 2016's token pools with stealing V and the practitioner defaults V (StraySpark).

BASE_CAP = {"melee": 3, "ranged": 2, "heavy": 1}       # per hero (design values)

class TokenPool:                                       # one per (hero, category)
    def __init__(self, hero, cat):
        self.hero, self.cat, self.holders = hero, cat, {}
    def cap(self):
        return max(1, round(BASE_CAP[self.cat] * director.aggression))   # 0.5x .. 1.5x
    def request(self, mob, score):                     # call on ENTERING attack state
        if mob in self.holders: return True
        if len(self.holders) < self.cap():
            self.holders[mob] = score; return True
        worst = min(self.holders, key=self.holders.get)
        if score > self.holders[worst] * 1.3:          # steal only if clearly better placed
            self.release(worst); worst.on_token_lost() # worst falls back to orbiting
            self.holders[mob] = score; return True
        return False
    def release(self, mob): self.holders.pop(mob, None)
    def audit(self):                                   # every ~2 s: reclaim leaked tokens
        for m in list(self.holders):
            if m.dead or m.target is not self.hero or m.stuck_time > 3.0:
                self.release(m)

3 · Boss phases plus weighted move selection (L5)

PHASES = [  # hp_floor: phase is active while hp_pct > floor; transitions are one-shot
  dict(name="P1", floor=0.70, moves={"Cleave": 5, "Charge": 3, "WarStomp": 2}),
  dict(name="P2", floor=0.35, moves={"Cleave": 3, "Charge": 3, "SummonAdds": 2, "Meteor": 3},
       on_enter=["bark:'You will kneel!'", "director.boss_budget(+400)"]),
  dict(name="P3", floor=0.00, moves={"Cleave": 2, "Meteor": 4, "Fixate": 3, "Charge": 2},
       on_enter=["rage(duration=20, dmg=1.1, speed=1.2, taken=0.9)"]),   # MH-style params
]

def boss_think(b, now):                     # called when the previous move FINISHES
    p = next(p for p in PHASES if b.hp_pct > p["floor"])
    if PHASES.index(p) > b.phase_idx:       # one-shot, never revert (healing can't retrigger)
        b.phase_idx = PHASES.index(p); run(p.get("on_enter", [])); return
    if now >= b.enrage_at:
        return do(b, "Berserk")             # hard enrage timer
    if b.state == "EXHAUSTED":
        return do(b, "Pant")                # post-rage punish window

    w = {}
    for m, base in p["moves"].items():
        mv = MOVES[m]
        if not ready(b, m) or not mv.in_band(dist_to_target(b)):   continue
        if mv.big and b.cd["shared_big"] > now:                    continue  # Classic WoW shared CD
        w[m] = base * (0.5 ** b.recent.count(m))                   # anti-repeat
    if count_heroes_in(b.pos, r=350) >= 3:
        w["WarStomp"] = w.get("WarStomp", 0) + 6                   # Roshan: punish clumping
    if b.grudge_hero_present:
        w["Fixate"] = w.get("Fixate", 0) * 2                       # memory / grudge

    m = weighted_pick(w, b.rng)             # seeded RNG => deterministic across clients
    telegraph(b, m, MOVES[m].windup)        # wind-up VFX + sound + bark
    schedule(MOVES[m].windup, execute, b, m)
    schedule(MOVES[m].windup + MOVES[m].recovery, boss_think, b)   # recovery = punish window
    if MOVES[m].big: b.cd["shared_big"] = now + 6.0
    b.recent.append(m); b.recent = b.recent[-3:]

# Rebuild shared maps once per second (0.5-2 s is the recommended band)
def rebuild_fields():
    hero_threat.clear(); mob_prox.clear(); danger.decay(0.5)
    for h in heroes:  hero_threat.stamp(THREAT_TPL[h.attack_range], h.pos, h.dps_norm)
    for m in mobs:    mob_prox.stamp(PROX_TPL[m.speed_class], m.pos, 1.0)
    for aoe in active_hero_aoes():  danger.stamp(DISC_TPL[aoe.radius], aoe.pos, 1.0)

def cell_value(mob, c):
    v  = ring(dist(c, mob.target.pos), preferred=mob.range, width=40)  # MSD ring: hold my range
    v -= 1.0 * hero_threat[c] * (mob.on_cooldown or mob.cowardice)     # kite only while reloading
    v -= 0.5 * mob_prox.excluding(mob)[c]                              # spacing, no blob
    v -= 3.0 * danger[c] * (mob.rng.random() < mob.dodge_chance)       # imperfect AoE dodging
    return v

def ranged_step(mob):                         # at 2-5 Hz; at most 9 evaluations
    best = max(neighbours8(mob.cell) + [mob.cell], key=lambda c: cell_value(mob, c))
    mob.move_to(best, delay=mob.rng.uniform(0.1, 0.4))                 # human-ish hesitation

def retreat_cell(mob):  return argmax_window(mob.pos, 8, lambda c: 1 - hero_threat[c])
def aoe_center(caster): return argmax_window(caster.pos, caster.cast_range, lambda c: hero_prox[c])

            heroes_clumped(r=350) / 4, healer_alive(), secs_since_big_move(b) / 10,
            b.rage_level, grudge_present(b)]

# --- Option A: local tiny MLP (e.g. 9 -> 64 -> N moves), int8 weights, ~microseconds ---
def mlp_pick(b):
    legal = legal_moves(b)
    logits = int8_mlp_forward(features(b))                 # fixed-point => lockstep-safe
    logits = [l if m in legal else -INF for m, l in zip(ALL_MOVES, logits)]   # ACTION MASK
    probs  = softmax(logits, temperature=b.personality_temp)                  # Boltzmann pick
    return sample(probs, b.rng)

# --- Option B: remote decision model (Jev-style), host-only, async, never blocking ---
def remote_pick_async(b):
    legal = legal_moves(b)
    req = {"question": "Which move should the boss use now?",
           "options": legal, "facts": describe(features(b))}  # text facts + option list
    b.pending = decision_api.request(req, timeout_ms=800)     # illustrative pseudo-API

def boss_choose(b):
    if b.pending and b.pending.ready():
        choice = b.pending.result().option
        b.pending = None
        if choice in legal_moves(b):           # VALIDATE: state may have changed in flight
            return choice
    return weighted_fallback(b)                # Pseudocode 3 never stops working

# Host decides, then broadcasts "boss uses X on Y" as a normal command => deterministic peers.
# Authored overrides beat the model (SupCom 2 ACU fix): e.g. force "Berserk" at enrage timer.

Which combination to ship first I

Checking the prototype plan against the research

The plan under consideration (mobs: FSM + utility; elites: BT + squad AI; bosses: HFSM + utility + memory; experimental: local neural policy or Jev-style layer choosing among legal actions) matches the research consensus and the recommended stack. Additions from the supplement I:

  1. Add attack tokens / circle of hate to the mob layer: the single highest-value fairness tool (DOOM 2016, Jurney GDC 2010).
  2. Add a director with a credit budget above the waves (L4D, Risk of Rain 2) and change difficulty only on enemies not yet on screen (Hunicke).
  3. For bosses, insert a contextual bandit or Dynamic Scripting layer before trying a neural model: adaptivity with determinism.
  4. For the experimental boss, prefer an open local decision model (d1-3B at ~8 ms on an RTX 4090, vendor figure, or a distilled tiny MLP or transformer) over a hosted API; call it asynchronously every 1–2 s, host-only, with a weighted-random fallback.

Learnings and anti-patterns from thirty years of beaten AIs

Humans exploit repeated scripts within a few games

"Humans are very adept at recognizing scripted behaviors and exploiting them to the fullest" V. ZZZKBot's 4-pool "was always the same", so a human scouted it, built cannons, and watched it feed Zerglings into them V. A best-response search beat every fixed combat script 0.95–1.00 V.

Do: keep boss structure learnable, add weighted variety inside it, and rotate wave tactics with Dynamic Scripting.

Yet strong simple scripts are the benchmark to beat

In microRTS the scripted POLightRush was "the most challenging opponent" every year V (microPhantom), and CIG 2015 was won by a single 4-pool rush V.

Do: write excellent scripts first; add search or learning only where scripts visibly fail.

Perceived intelligence beats real intelligence

F.E.A.R.'s barks V; "Tougher = Smarter" in Halo playtests V; Howland's "If it looks right, it is right" V.

Do: spend on telegraphs, barks, animation and pain reactions before algorithms.

Make good mistakes, never bad ones

A missed slam with a long recovery delights players. Walking into walls breaks immersion V.

Avoid: perfect AoE dodging, perfectly synchronized swings, instant 180° retargeting, single-file queues, pixel-perfect infinite kiting I.

Adjust difficulty only on enemies not yet on screen

Reactive changes look "schizophrenic" V. Players noticed speed and damage changes but accepted count changes V. L4D adjusts "pacing, not difficulty" V.

Never escalate because players died

Alien: Isolation's unlocks are never tied to player deaths, to avoid unfairness V.

Don't attack when the player cannot respond

DOOM's glory-kill lock and Arkham's takedown lock V. Elden Ring reacts to animations, not button presses V.

Label your cheats, or make them diegetic

SC2 names them "Cheater 1 (Vision)", "Cheater 2 (Resources)", "Cheater 3 (Insane)" V. AoE IV promised no resource cheats, and a player later claimed Hardest gets 2× gather: unconfirmed, but it shows how hidden cheats erode trust Vfor the dispute. C&C's AI ignored fog of war but mined like the player V. Alien's director knows where you are, but the alien must still find you V.

Commitment reads as courage

Bots that retreat whenever a simulator predicts a loss look broken (tscmoo) or timid (the Sejong pros) V. Utility AI needs commitment bonuses to stop dithering V.

Every learned component needs an authored leash

Drivatar's control layer reduced aggression and prevented rams; SupCom 2 needed the ACU override; Sophy was tuned for sportsmanship; the Jev Pokémon run leaned on scripted milestones and loop protection V.

Determinism for lockstep

Seeded RNG for every weighted pick; integer or fixed-point math for anything that crosses a decision threshold; run any float NN, cloud model or SLM only on the host and broadcast its decisions as commands; a KI-style case base with integer metrics is deterministic by construction. All I: no public post-mortem of neural-net desync in a lockstep RTS was found.

Bound every behavior

C&C hunters give up when a target stays out of range; WC3 creeps return past a distance; WoW bosses reset outside their arena V. Give every state a timeout.

Don't let engine defaults fight your design

The WC3 return-to-spawn bug came from melee creep-camp leash rules colliding with wave marching V. Separate leashed camps from objective-driven wave marchers explicitly.

Personality beats competence for long-term fun

AoE2 DE's AI is "effective but dull", and players asked for aggressor or builder personalities V. PA tunes difficulty as "overconfidence or timidness" V. GTAI's ladder changes behavior (harassment, drops) at each step, not just stats V.

Treat the team as one player and break its plan

Booth: "We treated the entire survivor team as one player". The Smoker exists to break perfect SWAT-team movement V.

Build debugging in from day one

L4D's behavior transitions carry a human-readable reason string; Guerrilla built in-game HTN decomposition views V. For utility and boss weights, show the scores above each unit in a debug overlay I.

Conclusion

The thirty-year arc reverses the usual hype story. Each new generation of AI technique added a new kind of selector on top of authored behavior rather than replacing it. FromSoftware still writes Lua state machines. DOOM's demons feel fierce because of a token counter. SupCom 2's neural nets were only trusted with one question. Jev beat Pokémon only after code reduced the game to a menu of legal choices. For a Hero Defense, the design problem is less "which algorithm is smartest?" and more "what menu do I author, who gets to choose from it, at what rate, and how does the player see the choice being made?" Simple, fast and reliable live in the menu and the throttles. Soul lives in the telegraph, the bark, the grudge, the punish window and the morale break.

That framing also tells you where new models fit. The September–October 2026 decision models finally make a neural selector practical at exactly one point: the boss brain's selection step, choosing among masked, telegraphed, authored moves every second or two, asynchronously, on the host, with a weighted table as a permanent fallback. Below that layer, every mob should stay deterministic, cheap and legible. The most distinctive version of your game will probably come from Dynamic Scripting, bandits and KI-style memory applied to bosses across attempts, not from deep RL or world models. Players will remember the warlord who learned to punish their tower-hugging, who taunted the paladin who killed him last time, and who left them a two-second window after his meteor.
