    1 · Fixed step, input and the verb machine

    The world advances in steps of 1/120 s from an accumulator, and the renderer draws between the last two steps. Every button is sampled once per step as a held bit; a press is the edge of its bit, taken inside the step, so a tap between frames still counts and the replay sees the same edge. The input record is one byte per step: left, right, up, down, jump held, katana held, dash pressed, climb held, run-length encoded as (byte, run) pairs. One level clock drives every mover, timed block, enemy and turret, which is why two runs with the same inputs are the same run. The verb state machine sits on top: one verb at a time, each with a startup, an active and a recovery phase, and a cancel table that says which input may interrupt which phase (jump cancels a slide and a wall run, letting go of the katana ends a hook, a wall ends a dash-slash, a kill refunds it).

    2 · Movement kit

    Unchanged from Mechanics #1. SpeedRunner's own numbers: 420 u/s top speed, 830 u/s jump, gravity 2500 with 1.65x on the way down, coyote 100 ms, buffer 130 ms, jump cut to 45%, apex hang, corner correction of 8 units, wall stick, wall coyote, roll after a 230 unit fall, and the speed tech (roll-jump hyper, lift boost, jump boost). Every verb above is built on these numbers and expressed as multiples of them.

    3 · Verb set

    Each verb is a row in one table: its input, its phase lengths, its speeds as multiples of the movement kit, and the resource it spends. The recommended set is the samurai's own: crouch and crawl (20 by 26 units, 120 u/s), the crouch-slide (from 280 u/s, friction 600, 120 ms minimum, release stands), the slide-jump (546 by 622, the roll-jump hyper's pair), the stomp (Down in the air sets the fall to 700 u/s), the automatic mantle (a top between the head and 16 units above it snaps the feet up with a 100 ms hop), the pogo slash (a 40 by 50 box under the feet, a full 830 bounce), the iai dash-slash (hold the katana 200 ms, release: 840 u/s for 100 to 150 ms with the slash box swept every step, 50 ms of locked recovery, one per airtime, refunded by a kill) and the ring hook (Up plus katana fires a hook along the 45 degree ray within 300 units; the rope is fixed at the attach distance, pump with left and right, let go of the katana to release at up to 760 u/s). The comparison verbs: dash at 2.67x for 150 ms with a 546 exit, air dash, double jump at 0.85x, dive at 600 u/s after a 120 ms wind-up, ground pound, wall run (92 units in 250 ms), climb at 210 u/s on a 2.4 s budget, glide at 0.25x terminal, the jetpack corridor (thrust 3500 u/s², terminal 600, auto-scroll, no fuel), the charged jump (0.6 to 1.5x from a standstill) and the rejected parry stance. The deflect belongs to the slash itself: any shot that enters the active box flies back at 1.5x.

    4 · Collision and elements

    Solids are axis aligned boxes resolved on x, then y, with a bounded loop so a squeeze never hangs. The hitbox is 20 by 46 units standing and 20 by 24 crouched; a low ceiling keeps you crouched until you clear it. New pieces for the gym: a crate that only a ground pound breaks, a hook point the grapple can reach, a turret that is solid until its own shot comes back at it, and spike-topped floors that bounce a pogo slash and kill anything else. One-way tiles, movers, springs and timed blocks carry over from the course unchanged.

    5 · Enemies and shots on level time

    The patroller walks its ledge at 85 units per second on the level clock, so it is in the same place at the same time in every attempt. The chaser spawns at the fourth checkpoint 1.5 s after you pass it and runs at 85% of your top speed; it drops but never climbs a wall over 150 units. The turret wakes when you are within 700 units and fires every 1.2 s on the same clock at 600 u/s (a slider from 300 to 1200), chest height; a slash that meets the shot reverses it at 1.5x and the reflected shot destroys the turret; the dash-slash's active steps are immune to shots. The pit enemies stand on spikes next to a spike-capped block and are the pogo targets. Slash is one button, 100 ms, usable in the air, never touches your speed.

    6 · Camera

    Locked to the player with 120 units of look-ahead that grows to 200 at overspeed, no lag, because a lagging camera hides the next obstacle at 420 units per second. Vertically it follows a reference that updates on landing and only climbs when the feet rise 60 units above it. A downward look-ahead of a quarter second of fall speed shows the landing. Try the speed look-ahead and the lerp follow, then the window overlay.

    7 · Animation and feedback

    The samurai is a stick rig with a hip height, a lean and eight limb angles, blended toward a target pose with 1 - exp(-26 dt). Every verb state has its own pose: the iai stance while the katana is held, the lunge with red afterimages every two steps, the low crouch and the laid-back slide, the feet-first stomp, the horizontal dive, the tucked ground pound, the hand plant of the mantle, the wall run lean, the climb with alternating arms, the hang on the rope with the hook drawn in flight, the glide with arms out, the jetpack tuck with a flame, the deep charge crouch, and the raised blade of the parry stance with a gold ring on a deflect. The stamina bar sits above the head only while the climb is on and turns red under 20%. There is no world hitstop, only the 120 ms sash flash. Squash, stretch, dust, speed lines and the camera carry over from #1.

    8 · Audio

    Every sound is a WebAudio oscillator or a noise burst with the game's frequencies, detuned by up to 4% per play with a cap of 8 voices. Each verb adds one synth sound: a swept noise for the dash, a short chirp for the double jump, a scrape for the slide and the climb, a low thud for the pound, a twang for the grapple, an airy hiss for the glide, a buzz per thrust burst for the jetpack, a rising tone for the charge, a metallic clang for the parry. The music loop keeps playing across deaths.

    9 · Race loop, leaderboard and trust

    The timer counts physics steps and starts on your first input. R resets in place. Splits show the delta against your session best. The canonical record of a run is its input log, one byte per step with the eight held bits, run-length encoded; the drawn ghost is derived from it at 15 Hz. The board stores the verb set a time used as its category, the way speedrun.com separates categories, and shows it next to the name. The validation panel hashes the movement numbers, the verb table and the verb toggles at the start of the run and checks the hash at the finish, then re-simulates the recorded inputs with the same code, verbs included, and compares the final time and position: PASS or MISMATCH. The speed cap follows the category's verb set. The verb budget readout counts the buttons the current set needs (Jump, Katana and Move, plus the Dash and Climb buttons when those verbs are on) and lists every verb the set gives the player.
