X-ray view (the data behind the pixels)

1 · Wetness is one byte per pixel

Raindrops raise a pixel's wetness. Each pixel draws its wet palette variant once wetness passes its own fixed Bayer-plus-noise threshold. Drizzle therefore speckles the ground first, and steady rain soaks it fully. Drying runs the same threshold backwards, so metal and stone dry fastest and cloth and dirt slowest. In drizzle, drying keeps pace, so the ground stays patchy.

2 · Puddles = water depth + infiltration

A second buffer holds standing water. It flows to lower neighbours, as the acid did. Each material takes a different share of the rain and drains it at its own rate, part fixed and part in proportion to depth: sand and grass soak it up, while dirt ruts, potholes and dips in the cobbles hold it. Puddles therefore appear on paths and plazas, never on the lawn.

3 · Ripples: two height buffers

The pond, the fountain and every puddle deep enough share one ripple simulation (Hugo Elias): new = (n+s+e+w)/2 − old, damped by 0.95. Each drop that lands on water pushes one pixel down. Rings are shaded from the ripple slope in one or two palette steps.

4 · Drops have height, wind has curtains

Each streak is a ground point plus a height z, drawn slanted by the wind. It splashes according to the surface it lands on: rings on water, small crown droplets on stone and wood, and mud on soaked dirt. A scrolling noise field drives drop density, so sheets of heavier rain sweep across with the wind.

5 · Shelter, rain shadow, drips

Exposure is baked per wind bucket, as in the snow study. Eaves, the awning and tree canopies stay dry, and walls throw a dry rain shadow downwind. Roofs, trees and the awning store water and release it as drips. Drips fill a gravel trench under the eaves and keep falling well after the rain stops.

6 · People, fire, lightning

Characters soak up rain unless they're under shelter or the umbrella. They drip, dry near the fire and leave wet footprints on dry floor; muddy footprints sink and fill with water. The villager walks to the awning once the rain reaches moderate. Rain smothers the campfire into steam. Storms add midpoint-displacement bolts and a delayed thunder shake, with a reduce-flashes option.

7 · Two kinds of mist

A 160×90 mist grid gets spray haze wherever heavy rain hits hard surfaces and water. When the sun returns, soaked ground gives off evaporation mist that drifts upward. Both are drawn as a dithered palette veil, the study's single fog interaction.
