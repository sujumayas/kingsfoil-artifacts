1 · The server grid

One cell per 32 px tile, 20 by 12 = 240 cells. Each cell stores material, fuel, moisture, state (fresh, catching, burning, embers, ash), the side the fire came from (3 bits) and the tick it caught. The server ticks at 4 Hz for water, rain and events. A burning cell rolls for spread once per second, counted from the tick it caught, so tiles stagger by themselves. Catching is only a look. The first roll comes one second after the tile caught. A roll uses VFX #6's chance per neighbour: green grass 0.15, dry grass 0.40, tree 0.22, wood 0.30, fence 0.30, hay 0.50, oil 1.0, times 1 minus moisture, times the Alexandridis wind term, diagonals times 0.7, decided by hash(x, y, tick, seed). A cell burns for a fixed number of rolls: green grass 5, dry grass 2, hay 4, oil 6, fence 12, tree 24 and wood wall 30, which are seconds for a 32 px tile. The 16 px and 8 px grids roll 2 and 4 times a second, so their cells burn a half and a quarter as long and the front keeps its speed in px per second. On a uniform field with no wind it moves 0.47, 0.52 and 0.51 tiles per second through green grass at 32, 16 and 8 px, and 0.91, 0.90 and 0.91 through dry grass. The wind slider is V in m/s, 0 to 10. At 5 m/s the chance per roll is 1.25 times higher downwind and 0.34 times upwind, so a green grass front runs at 0.53 tiles per second with the wind and mostly dies against it. Embers last 4 to 10 s, and ash regrows after 60 to 120 s in two steps.

2 · What goes over the wire

The HUD compares two encodings each tick. Sending the full burning set costs 2 bytes per burning cell. Sending only ignitions and extinguishes costs 3 bytes per event, because clients derive burnout from the fuel table and the ignition tick. Both add a 4 byte tick header, and the HUD shows the sum over one second. The sizes are illustrative, not measured on a real server.

3 · The burn front at art resolution

Each cell keeps its ignition time. Every 2 by 2 px block samples those times bilinearly between cell centres, through a fixed warp of up to 8 px, so the front crosses tile borders without a seam. A cell that has not caught counts as now plus 6 s, which slows the front at its edge, so a tile that never catches keeps a ragged rim of grass. A painted burn-order map jitters the arrival by up to 0.9 s, so char arrives in clusters. The map is 128 px and tileable, grown from 96 seed blobs and stored in 2 by 2 px blocks. The edge is 2 px, a glow pixel on the outer side of each border block and a dark red pixel inside it. Behind it comes a dark scorch zone 0.35 s wide, then char. When a cell runs out of fuel its clock stops, so pixels the front never reached stay grass. Ash spreads the same way from a second field of ash times. Per tile uses a ramp from the entry side plus the same map and shows the tile seam. Bayer 4 is the VFX #6 look. Whole tile flips at once and is cheapest. Burning cells redraw at 30 Hz and embers at 15 Hz into a cached base. Every other pixel is a copy.

4 · Flames

All loops are built in code. Candle 4x8 px with 4 frames, post torch 10x16 with 6 frames at 100 ms, hand torch 6x8 with 4 frames, burning grass 8x10 and 12x16, brazier 16x20 with 6 frames at 110 ms, campfire 24x28 with 8 frames at 110 ms, tree 22x28 with 8 frames at 120 ms and house 28x32 with 8 frames at 130 ms. They use 4, 5 or 6 tones that bend from purple through red to cream. The bottom 3 rows hold still in every frame, and a 2x2 or 2x3 px tongue breaks off and rises 3 px per frame. Each flame takes its phase from x × 3047 + y × 88. Post torches share one clock and fire fields run their own from the start of each stage. A burning tile shows up to 3 flames where its front has passed. For the last quarter of its burn it keeps 1 small flame that loops 1.4 times slower. DOOM 1:1 and 1:2 run Sanglard's in-place loop from the same emitters, with a seed level taken from the flame height. A 3 by 3 box average comes before the cut into 5 tones with a small cream core.

5 · Embers, sparks and smoke

One fixed pool of 512 particles. Embers are 2x2 px and rise for 0.5 to 1.5 s, coloured by age. Sparks are 1x1 or 1x2 px and live 3 to 8 frames in the two hottest tones. Embers 1 px is the VFX #6 look. Smoke, steam and oil soot are byte fields with 4, 8 or 16 px cells, advected at 30 Hz. They rise 26 px per second, drift 4 px per second per step of wind and keep 50% per second (steam 16%). A burning tile pours smoke into the 16x16 px at its top, twice as much while it dies. The field draws through a 2x2 px cluster map of soft blobs, or Bayer 4. Objects add drawn puffs, at most 64 alive. Campfire puffs grow from 4 to 6 px to 16 to 20 px every 0.5 s, chimney puffs from 8 to 10 px to 32 to 47 px every 0.6 s, tree puffs from 10 to 12 px to 32 to 40 px and house puffs from 12 to 16 px to 48 to 64 px in dark grey. Water makes bursts of white puffs.

6 · Trees and houses

A tree is the fuel of its trunk tile. While it catches, one flame burns at the base. Then 3 flames climb the crown over the first third of the burn. It ends as a bare charred tree, and 20 s later only a stump is left. A house holds fuel in each of its 9 tiles but draws as one object from the share that has burnt. Smoke leaks from under the eaves while it catches. From 25% flames break through the roof. At 60% the roof falls in to charred rafters with a burst of embers and smoke. At 100% charred wall stumps remain, and the stone chimney stands. The stone house never burns. Every object keeps three drawn looks for each pixel, normal, charred by rule from the same art, and the ruin.

7 · Combat fire

A missile flies for 150 × √d ms, d in tiles, as one still 16x16 px sprite picked from 8 directions. The fireball lays a 1-tile fire effect on each tile of a 3 by 3 area, or on the 37 tiles of the great fireball. The effect has 14 frames, 40 ms then 13 × 60 ms, on a 56 px canvas that spills past its tile. The centre goes first and each ring follows 75 ms later, with a variant picked by position. A tile refuses a repeat younger than 350 ms. Per-tile scorch marks fade over 4.5 s. Fire field and fire wall run Tibia's three stages of 200, 148 and 98 s, scaled here to 20, 14.8 and 9.8 s, with 5 frames of 120 ms then 100 ms. Their flame boxes are 46x46, 44x50 and 34x36 px, anchored in the tile's bottom-right corner, and the last stage is harmless. Entering a field costs 20 hit points. A burning actor loses 10 a second while its stacks last, and water clears them. Shake is 2 px for a 3 by 3 blast and 5 px for the great fireball.

8 · Characters at 24x32

The sprites are the snow-32px ones, with 4 walk frames, steps on frames 0 and 2 and 400 ms per tile. The hand torch is a 2x8 px stick with a 6x8 px flame, held 2 to 3 px out from the hand and moved 1 px per walk frame. It draws behind the body only when facing north, and its top 2 rows lean 1 px against the walk. Rim light looks for the nearest fire within 3 tiles and rounds its direction to 8 steps. A pixel turns warm where the next pixel toward the fire is empty, and a second pixel takes a warm mid tone within 1.5 tiles. A character north of the fire gets none. A second shadow stretches away from the fire. Burning tints the palette row (R × 1, G × 0.7, B × 0.6) and adds 8x10 flames at the shoulder and hip, a 6x8 flame at the head every other second, 7 embers a second and a 4 px grey puff every 0.5 s. A hit flashes white for 3 frames, plays a 9 × 100 ms ring of flame and shows an 11 px orange number that rises 48 px in 1 s. Hits within 400 ms merge. Soot prints of 3x5 px, 2 per tile, follow for 3 tiles after ash and fade over 10 s.

9 · Light, palette and haze

The world draws into a palette index buffer. Its 97 colours have 4 versions each (ambient, warm 1, warm 2 and the burning tint), and day, dusk and night retint those 388 entries once per frame. Lava and the pond glints cycle palette entries every 300 and 450 ms, so they cost no pixel work. Fires, torches and the lava add pools into a light map of 32, 8 or 4 px cells, with falloff (1 − d²/r²)² and flicker from VFX #5's 10 Hz light-style strings. The map draws in two warm bands, at 0.3 and 0.62, through a 2x2 px cluster dither 0.026 wide. Blocks whose band cannot change are written as flat runs. Smoke counts 1.8 times brighter, so it glows above fires. Heat haze shifts rows by 1 px above burning objects, fields and the campfire.

What it costs

Every layer is timed with performance.now() each frame and smoothed, and the bar shows the total against 16.7 ms. The HUD counts burning cells, spread rolls and neighbour checks per tick, burn-front pixels drawn, DOOM cells swept, smoke field cells and bytes, pool use, light map cells and the bytes Godot's Compatibility renderer would upload, which is whole textures every time. Cost view colours each pixel by how often it was written this frame. window.fire32.bench(n) runs n frames back to back and returns the mean ms per layer.

What changed from VFX #6

The canvas has 4 times the pixels and a tile has 16 times. A 1 px ember, a 4 tone 8 px flame, a Bayer burn and a Bayer smoke were sized for 8 px cells. Press VFX #6 settings to see them at this scale, then 32 px defaults. The spread grid did not have to grow. The server keeps one cell per tile and rolls once a second, and the burn front, flames, smoke and light carry the detail at art resolution on the client.
