1 · Climate picks the forest

Two low-frequency noises give every point a temperature (colder toward the north) and a moisture. A Whittaker-style table turns the pair into a forest type, and each type has its own density, species weights and undergrowth. Species weights are also multiplied by their own slow noise, so birches gather into groves instead of salt-and-pepper mixing.

2 · Blue-noise placement

Bridson's Poisson-disk sampling scatters candidate spots at least 9 px apart across the whole map. Each forest type keeps a share of them (density) and the path clears a strip. The result looks natural, with no grid lines and no clumps touching, and costs O(n) once at load.

3 · Few sprites, thousands of trees

At load, 12 species × 6 size/shape variants × 4 seasons are drawn procedurally into small canvases: leaf clusters lit from the top-left, bark textures, bare branches by recursion, and snow on conifers. Every tree is a reference to one of those sprites, so drawing a tree is a few cheap drawImage calls (three sway bands and a trunk, plus its shadow). The sprites are packed below for viewing only. In Godot you would pack them into one real atlas texture so they batch.

4 · Only what the camera sees

Trees sit in a 64 px spatial grid. Each frame reads only the cells under the camera, plus a margin for tall crowns. It y-sorts those few hundred items with the player and draws them back to front. Perched birds ride on their tree and flying birds are drawn above everything. Turn on Culling grid to watch the cells being used.

5 · Wind as row offsets

Each crown is drawn in 3 horizontal bands, each shifted by a whole number of pixels: top ×2.2, middle ×1.4, low ×0.7, trunk fixed. The sway follows a gust wave that travels across the map with the wind, so you see waves roll over the canopy. Conifers are stiff and palms and birches flex. It is the scanline-scroll trick from 16-bit consoles.

6 · Behind, beside, beneath

If a crown in front of you overlaps your sprite, the game swaps in a pre-dithered copy (a 50 % checkerboard), clips a circle out of it, or draws your x-ray silhouette on top. Trunks block movement while bushes slow you and rustle. With the Shadows layer on, standing inside a leafy tree's shadow ellipse darkens your sprite, and sunflecks drift in the shade.

7 · Seasons, tree by tree

Each tree adds its own phase offset to the global season: maples turn early, oaks late, and each individual varies a bit. As the year advances, the forest changes one tree at a time. The ground is re-baked once per season: snow under open sky but less under dense canopy (in the colder half of the map), leaf litter in autumn, and flowers in spring and summer.

8 · Birds

Birds perch on crowns and hop. They take off when you come within about 30 px or shake their tree, flock with boid rules (separation, alignment and cohesion, plus a pull toward a point near you and a speed limit) and land again on a tree away from you. Flying birds cast a ground shadow offset by their height. Crows show up in winter and parrots in the rainforest.
