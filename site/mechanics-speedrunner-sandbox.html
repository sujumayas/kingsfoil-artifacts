    1 · Fixed step and input

    The world advances in steps of 1/120 s from an accumulator, and the renderer draws between the last two steps. A jump press is a one-shot flag that the next step consumes, so a tap between frames still counts. One level clock drives every mover, timed block and enemy, which is why two runs with the same inputs are the same run. The 60 Hz toggle halves the step rate so you can feel the coarser arcs.

    2 · Movement kit

    These are SpeedRunner's own numbers. Coyote time keeps a jump legal for 100 ms after a ledge, the buffer holds a press for 130 ms until the feet land, and letting go of jump cuts the rise to 45%. Apex hang softens gravity near the top, fall gravity is 1.65x on the way down, and corner correction nudges you around a block edge by up to 8 units instead of stopping you dead. Wall reach finds a wall 7 units away, wall speed keep carries your run speed into a wall jump for 60 ms, ceiling grace keeps a jump alive through a bump in its first 50 ms. Roll-jump hyper, lift boost and jump boost are the speed tech. Switch each one off and run the steps again.

    3 · Collision and elements

    Solids are axis aligned boxes resolved on x, then y, with a bounded loop so a squeeze never hangs. One-way tiles only catch feet coming from above, movers carry whoever stands on them, springs launch at 1.45x jump speed as an automatic jump with the apex band active, and timed blocks run a 0.5 s tick on a four tick cycle, 1 s there and 1 s gone, from one sequence integer each. Each element shows what it is about to do. A block shakes for the last 0.3 s before it vanishes, a spring stays compressed for 150 ms, spikes are silhouettes you can read at speed.

    4 · Enemies on level time

    The patroller walks its ledge at 85 units per second on the level clock, so it is in the same place at the same time in every attempt, and a touch only kills after two overlapping steps on a hitbox inset by 3 units. The chaser spawns at the fourth checkpoint 1.5 s after you pass it and runs at 85% of your top speed, which turns any hesitation into a visible cost. Slash is one button, 100 ms (17 ms wind-up, 33 ms active, 50 ms recovery), a 50 by 40 unit box ahead, usable in the air, cancelled by a jump, and it never touches your speed. Landing on the patroller gives a full jump with the apex band.

    5 · Camera

    The default camera is locked to the player with 120 units of look-ahead and no lag, because a lagging camera hides the next obstacle at 420 units per second. Vertically it follows a reference that updates on landing and only climbs when the feet rise 60 units above it, so a normal jump does not shake the view. Try the speed based look-ahead and the lerp follow, then switch on the window overlay to see the dead zone.

    6 · Animation and feedback

    The samurai is a stick rig with a hip height, a lean and eight limb angles, blended toward a target pose with 1 - exp(-26 dt), about 38 ms of lag. A jump stretches the figure to (0.6, 1.4), a landing squashes it toward (1.4, 0.6) in proportion to the fall speed, and both recover at 1.75 scale units per second. Rolls and flips spin the whole figure. Dust is 7 puffs on a jump, 6 on a landing faster than 575 units per second, 10 on a roll and one every 90 ms while running. The sash flashes for 120 ms when a jump, wall jump, roll, spring or stomp changes what you can do next. Speed lines appear above 90% of top speed. The Juice slider scales all of it.

    7 · Audio

    Every sound is a WebAudio oscillator or a noise burst with the game's frequencies. Jump sweeps 420 to 760 Hz, death 300 to 70 Hz, checkpoint 880 to 1320 Hz, the finish plays 523, 659, 784 and 1046 Hz. Each play is detuned by up to 4% and at most 8 voices overlap. The music loop is 4 bars and keeps playing across deaths. Switch "restart music on death" on to hear how a reset makes every death feel like a loading screen.

    8 · Race loop

    The timer counts physics steps, never the wall clock, so a slow frame cannot change a time. The timer and the level clock start together on your first horizontal or jump input, so waiting at the line gains nothing. The countdown plays only on the first attempt after a map select, R resets in place, and any input skips the 0.38 s death beat. "Countdown replay" reproduces the game today, 0.38 s dead plus a 3 s countdown, 3.4 s per death. Splits show the delta against your session best in LiveSplit colours, two decimals in the run and three on the results. The canonical record of a run is its input log, one byte per step, run-length encoded. The drawn ghost is a state track derived from it at 15 Hz, and the byte counter shows what each choice costs. Medals follow Trackmania ratios on the 20 s author run, gold 21.2 s, silver 24 s, bronze 30 s.

    9 · Leaderboard and trust

    Five friends are seeded on the board and your time slots in among them, with a toast when you pass one. The board shows rank, gap in ms, date and input device. The validation panel runs the checks a server would run. Step count against time, a minimum step count from the map length, a slowdown ratio of wall clock over simulated time flagged above 1.05, a minimum time per split from the distance, a speed cap, and a full input replay that re-simulates your recorded inputs with the same code and compares the final time. Slow motion and the other assists mark the run as assisted and the board rejects it. Share link puts the run summary in the URL.
