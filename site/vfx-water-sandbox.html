Walk with the arrows or WASD, or tap where the walker should go. Pick a tool and click the water. Keys: 1-5 tools, T tide auto, V next view, Space pause. The tide runs on a 2 minute clock (illustrative) and only its byte goes over the wire. With the rod, click the water to cast and click again when the bobber dips.

Simulation

1 · Server water truth

The coast is a 40 by 23 grid of 8 px cells. Each cell has a height, a water kind (shallow, deep, river, swamp, waterfall), a flow direction with speed 0 to 3 and a fishable flag. Shallow water slows you to 0.6, deep water blocks you like Tibia or lets you swim. One tide byte moves the shoreline: a cell is wet when its height sits under the level, so beach rows flip as it rises and falls.

2 · Distance and depth

When the tide byte changes the client runs one multi-source BFS from the shore cells. That gives a signed distance for every cell, plus in water and minus on land. Depth per pixel mixes that distance with the height under the water level and picks one of 5 sea bands, 3 river bands or 3 swamp bands. The HUD shows what the recompute costs.

3 · Colour and surface

Each body has its own hue-shifted ramp, teal in the shallows down to navy, with a 4 by 4 Bayer dither at band borders and no alpha. Palette cycle rotates 4 reserved slots per band at 8 Hz, so the pixels never change and only the palette moves. Animated tiles play a 4 frame loop at 5 fps with a hashed phase per cell. Turn on Sync tile phase to see the grid shimmer that the phase offset hides.

4 · Flow

River pixels read a flow vector filtered from the cell grid and scroll a noise texture along it. Two samples run half a cycle apart with triangle weights, so the texture never stretches and the reset never shows. Fast cells turn the highlights white. The waterfall is a strip that scrolls down at 55 px/s with churn and mist particles at its base.

5 · Shore and waves

The coast uses dual-grid marching squares. The 4 corner cells give one of 16 cases, and only mixed cases (1 to 14) and their neighbours do foam work. A swash line runs up the beach every 5 s, breaks into foam and pulls back past the tide line. It leaves wet sand that dries over about 6 s. Wave crests come in ahead of it.

6 · Reflections and caustics

Trees, the hut and the walker draw a mirrored copy below their base line. Each row shifts by a sine wobble of up to 1 px, the copy is mixed with the water colour through the palette, and it is clipped to water pixels. Shallow water gets caustics, two scrolling Voronoi edge textures with a 1 px line where either edge is close.

7 · Interaction

Wading clips the sprite at the waterline and drops a ring each step. Moving in water spawns wake pairs that spread sideways at 0.354 of your speed, which is tan 19.5°, the Kelvin half-angle. Rings live in a fixed pool of 64 squashed ellipses and wake bits in a pool of 256. Logs bob 1 px at 0.5 Hz and ride the current to the sea. Pond heightfield swaps the pond rings for a coarse two-buffer ripple grid, the one from VFX #3 Rain.

8 · Palette and light

Every pixel is a palette index until the last step. Day, dusk and night swap the palette, not the pixels. Night darkens the water ramp further and adds a moon glint column. Dusk gets a warm sun glint. The hut window has its own slot and lights up after dark.
