1 · Light = albedo × light map

The world is drawn once as plain colours, with no shading of its own. Each frame builds a light map: sky ambient, plus the sun where nothing blocks it, plus every point light. Each pixel then multiplies its colour by that light. Shadows are bluish because only the sky lights them, and lamplit spots are warm, so you get warm light and cool shadows without painting them.

2 · Sun shadows by projecting heights

Each object pixel knows how far it stands above its own base line (3/4 view). It casts a dot at base + sunDir × height × cot(elevation), so silhouettes project like real shadows. The length is scaled by 0.62 for the 3/4 view, and the cotangent is capped so dawn and dusk shadows stay on screen. Roof and crown pixels are first moved back to the ground spot they stand over, so houses and trees cast filled footprints rather than streaks. At noon they are short and point north. At golden hour they are long, and they swing from west to east across the day; the obelisk is a sundial. A few canopy pixels let light through as sunflecks. At night the moon casts faint blue shadows.

3 · Point-light shadows: a polar depth map

Each shadow-casting light marches 256 rays through a ground-level occluder-height grid and stores the hit distance per angle, a 1D shadow map. A pixel is lit if its distance from the light is no more than that angle's hit distance. An occluder blocks if it is at least 60 % of the light's height, so a low fence stops the campfire but not the street lamp. Lights cache their rays and recompute only when someone walks near.

4 · Personality = colour + flicker + reach

Each kind of light has its own colour and rhythm. Candle: almost steady, since a single flame in still air barely flickers; real 10–12 Hz flicker needs 3 or more flames. It dips now and then in a draught. Torch and campfire: fast noise flicker with a breathing radius. Lantern: steady, swaying with your stride. Oil street lamp: almost steady. Magic crystal: slow cyan↔violet pulse. Rune lamp: cold, steady, with sudden electric blackouts. Mushrooms breathe slowly, and fireflies blink. Tiny lights skip shadows, which costs almost nothing.

5 · Buildings: inside vs outside

Walls are tall occluders with gaps where the door and windows are. By day, sun reaches the interior only through those gaps, so you see moving sun patches on the floor. At night the fireplace and candle shine out through the same gaps, throwing warm windows and door light onto the grass. Houses light up at dusk in a staggered order. The left house's lamps go out around 23:24 but its banked fireplace glows all night, and the right house goes dark just after midnight.

6 · Pixel-art light styles

Smooth RGB is plain multiply. Banded + dither rounds the light to steps of 1/6 per channel (more steps where it is over-bright) with a Bayer threshold, giving retro rings of light. DOOM colormap turns brightness into 16 shade levels per palette colour, with dark levels shifted toward blue-violet: one table lookup per pixel, but coloured lights turn grey.

7 · Water and characters

Lights near the pond lay shimmering vertical glints on the water, as do the moon at night and the low sun at golden hour. You and the lantern-bearer block point lights, so you cast long shadows away from the campfire, and the sun stamps a simple 5-px-wide strip for each character.
