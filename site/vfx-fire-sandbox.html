Simulation

1 · The grid is the truth

The village is a 40 by 23 grid of 8 px cells. Each cell has a material, fuel, moisture, a state (igniting, burning, embers, ash) and heat. Only burning cells sit on the active list, and the grid ticks at 4 Hz. Every roll uses hash(x, y, tick, seed), an integer hash, so every client that knows the seed computes the same fire in any order.

2 · Spread, wind and rain

Each tick a burning cell rolls once per neighbour: p = base × (1 − moisture) × wind, diagonals × 0.7. Base: grass 0.15, dry grass 0.40, tree 0.22, wood 0.30, oil 1.0. Wind is the Alexandridis term e^(V(0.045 + 0.131(cos θ − 1))), so a strong wind makes fire 1.6 times likelier downwind and 9 times less likely upwind. Rain wets fuel and puts out burning cells with the Minecraft chance, 20 % plus 3 % per age step, scaled to the 4 Hz tick.

3 · What goes over the wire

The HUD compares two encodings each tick. Sending the full burning set costs 2 bytes per burning cell. Sending only ignitions and extinguishes costs 3 bytes per event, because clients derive burnout from the fuel table. Both add a 4 byte tick header. The sizes are illustrative, not measured on a real server.

4 · Two flame styles

Sprite loop: 4 frame flames drawn from a 4 tone ramp (near-white, yellow, orange, deep red) at 10 fps, with the Stardew phase offset x × 3047 + y × 88 so neighbours never sync. House fires loop slower. DOOM buffer: a 1 px heat buffer where burning cells feed the bottom row, spread with the DOOM rule (random decay, random sideways step, biased by the wind) and drawn through its 37 colour palette.

5 · Embers, smoke and steam

Embers come from a fixed pool of 256 particles that rise, drag and drift with the wind, colour set by age. Smoke lives in an 80 by 45 field of 4 px cells, advected by the wind plus a steady rise, with decay. It is drawn with a 4 by 4 Bayer threshold, never alpha: grey smoke, white steam, near-black oil soot. The chimney puffs use the Stardew numbers: 0.5 px per frame rise at zoom 4, constant sideways push, growth and fade over about 6 s.

6 · Combat fire

The fireball flies with a trail, then explodes: 2 white flash frames, an expanding ring, sparks, a shake and smoke. It leaves a 3 by 3 fire field that weakens through 3 stages, like a Tibia fire field. Touching fire gives the sheep burn stacks. Each tick it takes a small hit, flashes red and loses one stack. Water clears it.

7 · Light, palette and haze

Burning cells add to a coarse glow grid that is blurred and flickers at about 10 Hz, then added warm on top of the ambient light. Night makes the fires carry the scene. Palette lock maps every final pixel to the 32 colours of Endesga 32 with an ordered dither. Heat haze shifts rows above big fires by a sine offset.
