X-ray view (the data behind the pixels)

1 · One float of acid per pixel

Acid is a single buffer (Float32Array(320*180)). Each step, every wet pixel sends a share of its surface height (acid plus floor height) to lower neighbours. A thin film below 0.3 can't wet dry pixels, which acts as cheap surface tension and gives puddles clean edges. A slightly lower drain trench guides spills toward the iron gate.

2 · Materials eat acid back

Every object pixel stores a material and an integrity value from 1 to 0. Acid touching it removes rate × concentration and is used up by the reaction, so a small spill stops on its own. Cloth goes fastest, then iron, leather and wood, then bone. Gold and stone are immune; glass flasks only shatter when struck. Solid things like crates, barrels and bars are eaten from their wet edge inward, one pixel at a time.

3 · Stages, not health bars

Integrity picks a colour stage through a Bayer-dithered threshold, so stages blend instead of popping. Iron goes clean → pitted (dark pits) → pale green salts → dull → holes. Wood darkens, chars to black, then crumbles. Once acid is gone, damaged iron slowly rusts, and the rust spreads to neighbouring iron.

4 · Slimes are the same field

A slime is a metaball: a smooth falloff blob that is squashed and stretched by a wobble phase. The renderer draws slime where slimeField + 0.55·acid ≥ 1, so a slime visibly melts into the puddles it crosses. Its core is see-through, so items it's digesting show inside. Slimes leave acid trails, grow by eating, split in two past 45 mass or when cut while heavier than 12 (up to a population cap), and burst into a pool when they die.

5 · Armor before flesh

Standing in acid wears your boots down first. Once they're gone, your HP drops with sizzle smoke and a flash. Slime contact eats the chestplate first. Your sword loses integrity on every slime it hits, and fumes slowly etch the helmet. Your boots also soak up acid and print acidic footprints that etch the floor.

6 · Fumes, stains and time

Evaporation and reactions feed a 160×90 fume grid that blurs, decays and drifts north. It's drawn as dithered green haze; dense fumes hurt. Shrinking puddle edges leave ring stains (the coffee-ring effect). Lime neutralizes acid into a white salt crust that fades over time.

7 · Light from the liquid

Every 4 frames, the acid is summed into 8×8 blocks and blurred twice, giving a green glow map. Each pixel then chooses between three palettes — dim, torch or acid-glow — through a Bayer threshold. That gives the dithered light pools of old dungeon crawlers at almost no cost.
